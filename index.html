RPKI now covers 70% of routes after China's surge

IPinfo / daily.dev · Oct 16, 2026 · Industry news

RPKI now secures 70.3% of global Internet routes, up from 54.0% a year earlier, with China's adoption leap (3.7% to 83.0%) driving most of the gain. However, RPKI only validates origin, not path: a August 2026 Hetzner hijack exploited a loosely scoped ROA to forge a route and serve malware, and roughly 16-21% of covered routes still have such 'open windows.'

Why it matters Of 855,217 RPKI-covered IPv4 routes, 138,698 (16.2%) have a ROA looser than the route announced; operators should leave maxLength unset and publish ROAs for all announced prefixes to close attack windows.

RPKI-valid routes cover 70.3% of routed prefixes, up from 54.0% a year earlier, with IPv4 addresses rising from 53.1% to 65.0%. Much of the jump came from China moving from 3.7% to 83.0% of its IPv4 addresses in valid routes, accounting for nearly 60% of the global gain. However, RPKI validates only the origin AS, not the path taken by the route, and cannot catch attackers who stay within published ROA constraints. Roughly 16.2% of IPv4 and 20.9% of IPv6 RPKI-covered routes have loose 'open windows'—ROAs broader than the routes announced, creating gaps exploitable by forged-origin hijacks. The August 2026 Hetzner incident demonstrated this: attackers exploited a loosely scoped ROA authorizing origination at /24 lengths within a /16 block, received RPKI-valid validation, and used the route to obtain a TLS certificate and serve malicious updates. Operators should publish ROAs for everything announced (three in ten routed prefixes remain not RPKI-valid), avoid setting maxLength (one in six covered IPv4 routes has a gap like Hetzner's), and verify ROAs are still present, as several operators including Saudi Telecom, SEACOM, and Orange Maroc have recently lost some of theirs.

DNS root key rollover breaks resolvers missing new anchor

Akamai · Oct 11, 2026 · Industry news

On October 11, 2026, the DNS root zone switched to signing with KSK-2024 (key tag 38696), replacing KSK-2017. Any validating resolver lacking this trust anchor fails to resolve both signed and unsigned domains, causing widespread SERVFAIL errors within 48 hours.

Why it matters RFC 5011 automated updates often fail on offline systems, read-only key stores, uninventoried embedded appliances, or outdated golden images; operators must verify key tag 38696 is in resolver config files and active in memory before the rollover takes effect.

On October 11, 2026, the DNS root zone stopped signing with KSK-2017 (key tag 20326, active since 2018) and switched to KSK-2024 (key tag 38696), only the second KSK change in DNSSEC history. Resolvers can cache the old key set for up to 48 hours, so failures manifest within two days as the cache expires. KSK-2024 was published in the root zone on January 11, 2025, giving resolvers 21 months to pick it up either automatically through RFC 5011 (watching the root for new keys and trusting one after 30 days residence) or through software updates. Both keys use RSA/SHA-256, so no algorithm change occurred. Operators running DNSSEC-validating resolvers must ensure their systems trust key 38696 beforehand; a missing trust anchor breaks DNS broadly across an entire resolver, while zone-specific DNSSEC issues affect individual domains. Organizations should verify their baseline DNS health before October 11 to rapidly distinguish local configuration issues from root key rollover outages during troubleshooting.

Why it matters Active threat to internet-facing FortiGate appliances with verified credential databases in use by organized actors; requires immediate detection of unauthorized access and credential rotation across all FortiOS versions.

IETF draft ties segment routing SIDs to guaranteed resources

IETF Datatracker · Oct 13, 2026 · Primary source

Active IETF draft (v18, May 2026 publication, expires November 2026) describing mechanisms to allocate network resources (bandwidth, compute, memory) to Segment Routing Identifiers (SIDs) for both SR-MPLS and SRv6, enabling resource-guaranteed policies and Network Resource Partitions.

Why it matters Network operators can now engineer SR policies with explicit resource reservations without RSVP-TE overhead, enabling deterministic slicing for 5G/6G enhanced VPN services and compute-aware routing without per-path state explosion.

The draft-ietf-spring-resource-aware-segments working group document (v18, expires Nov 16, 2026) defines how to associate network resource subsets (bandwidth pools, dedicated processor time, memory buffers) with individual SIDs or groups of SIDs. This extends SR beyond pure topology steering to include resource-aware path selection: a resource-aware SID retains its forwarding semantics but also identifies the set of underlay resources reserved for packets using that SID. The mechanism supports centralized controller-driven resource planning based on service traffic matrices and performance requirements, addressing a long-standing gap where SR policies lacked the resource guarantee capabilities of RSVP-TE. Implementations are already in production on Huawei ATN9XX and CX600 routers. For network architects, this enables declarative service slicing: define an enhanced VPN intent ("latency < 10ms, throughput > 10Gbps") and map it to resource-aware SR policies, with the controller allocating appropriate SID pools and advertising them via BGP-LS. The draft remains active; standards track completion is expected soon, making this practical for near-term deployment in tier-1 carrier and cloud networks planning deterministic slices.
