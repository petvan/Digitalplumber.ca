Selector launches agent that builds network dashboards from plain language

Selector AI blog · Oct 16, 2026 · Primary source

Selector launched Artist, an agentic NetOps component within Selector Foundry that generates live network dashboards from plain-language requests in seconds, eliminating manual dashboard backlog and query syntax requirements. The agent integrates with Selector's full-stack observability and AIOps foundation to support real-time incident response and NOC workflows.

Why it matters Addresses NOC dashboard request bottlenecks by enabling non-technical operators to generate event-correlation and incident-context views instantly, reducing response time in multi-domain network events.

Selector Foundry represents a shift from traditional AIOps dashboards to agentic NetOps, where specialized AI agents (Rosetta, Loom, TicketMaster, Herald, Artist, Sculptor) collaborate on incident investigation and remediation within one correlated data model. Artist specifically solves a workflow friction point: in war rooms and post-incident reviews, teams repeatedly request custom dashboard views (one incident, one application path, one device context, executive summaries) that arrive too late or require SQL expertise. Artist accepts requests like "show me edge-rtr-3 dependencies during this incident" and builds an interactive dashboard matching the request, refinable in conversation. The platform consolidates raw telemetry (metrics, logs, events, flows, streaming telemetry) through a harmonized data model supporting cross-domain correlation; Selector claims 1,412 signals across three alert clusters resolve to a single incident with root cause (device reboot) visible in the view. This moves network operations from "detect and search" to "detect and context-render automatically," critical for NOCs handling multivendor, multi-domain environments where alert storms obscure root causes across L1–L7.

IP Infusion's OcNOS adds support for Broadcom Tomahawk 6

Business Wire · Oct 14, 2026 · Vendor release

IP Infusion announced OcNOS-DC network operating system support for Broadcom Tomahawk 6-based platforms, extending support across 40+ open hardware platforms and doubling bandwidth to 102.4 Tbps for next-generation AI and cloud networks with production-grade RoCEv2, dynamic load balancing, and UEC compliance.

Why it matters OcNOS-DC on Tomahawk 6 provides production-grade lossless RoCEv2 and UEC support across multiple ODM platforms, reducing operational friction and training burden for hyperscalers deploying next-gen AI fabric at scale.

OcNOS-DC is a network operating system purpose-built for AI and HPC fabric operations with runtime support for RoCEv2, Priority Flow Control, ECN-based congestion control with DCQCN, dynamic load balancing (DLB) for avoiding hash-based flow collisions, and UEC compliance. Extension to Tomahawk 6 represents significant milestone: Tomahawk 6 delivers 2x throughput jump from Tomahawk 5 (51.2 to 102.4 Tbps), supports native 1.6T ports (up to 64 native 1.6T or 512 × 200G radix for scale-out), and includes options for 200G and 100G PAM4 SerDes depending on copper reach and port density. OcNOS-DC already ships on Tomahawk 4 and Tomahawk 5 in production. Tomahawk 6 support extends OcNOS to 40+ open hardware platforms, giving operators choice in switch ODMs (Micas, Arista, Edge-core, Delta, Wistron) while maintaining single network OS and fabric-control paradigm. Particularly valuable for neocloud providers building GPU data centers at scale who need standardized OS to reduce training and automation burden across heterogeneous hardware deployments.

RPKI now protects 70% of internet routes

IPinfo · Oct 16, 2026 · Industry news

RPKI-valid routing jumped from 54% to 70.3% in a year, driven largely by China's adoption. Nearly half of all invalid routes reach only a single vantage point, and when wrong networks announce addresses, 71% reach just one peer.

Why it matters Rapid RPKI deployment now protects majority of routes; operators should prioritize ROV implementation and ASPA path validation to keep pace.

RPKI-valid routing jumped from 54% to 70.3% in a year, driven largely by China's adoption. This marks significant acceleration in cryptographic routing security deployment. Of all routed IPv4 space not in RPKI-valid routes, nearly 60% is in the United States, and about one in four addresses belongs to the US Department of Defense. Nearly half of all invalid routes reach only a single vantage point; when wrong networks announce addresses, 71% reach only one. This isolation of invalid routes demonstrates ROV's practical effectiveness at blocking hijacks early. However, IPinfo found thousands of records flagged as failed that were present in every independently validated set they checked, with their routes still RPKI-valid, meaning a count of failing records is not a measure of lost protection. Path validation via ASPA is emerging as the next frontier, with deployment accelerating but still requiring broader router and validator support.

Timor-Leste builds national IXP with RPKI validation

APNIC Blog · Oct 16, 2026 · Analysis

Timor-Leste is establishing a national IXP (TL-IXP) with integrated RPKI validation, route servers, and DNS root mirrors. Approximately 60% of announced address space is covered by valid ROAs with 34% invalid-route filtering measured.

Why it matters Regional IXP deployment with integrated RPKI validation demonstrates how emerging regions build routing security into new infrastructure; operator readiness metrics show 34% ROV adoption can improve significantly.

The Timor-Leste Internet Exchange Point (TL-IXP) will allow ISPs and other networks to exchange traffic locally rather than routing through international connections. Plans include route servers, DNS root mirrors, monitoring infrastructure, and routing security measures such as RPKI validation. Timor-Leste has made progress in strengthening routing security through RPKI, with approximately 60% of announced address space covered by valid ROAs. ROAs allow network operators to declare which Autonomous Systems are authorized to originate specific IP address prefixes, and Route Origin Validation uses this information to identify and reject invalid route announcements. Over the seven days ending July 10, 2026, APNIC Labs measured invalid-route filtering in Timor-Leste at 34.42%, compared with 14.46% across Southeast Asia and 26.69% globally. While measurements indicate encouraging progress, continued ROA creation and broader implementation of ROV among network operators will be important as Timor-Leste's Internet ecosystem grows and becomes more interconnected.

CISA flags exploited root code execution flaw in PAN-OS

Help Net Security · Oct 15, 2026 · Industry news

Palo Alto Networks confirmed CVE-2026-0322, a CVSS 8.6 vulnerability in PAN-OS management interface allowing authenticated attackers with network access to execute arbitrary code with root privileges. CISA added it to KEV catalog with three-day federal remediation deadline.

Why it matters Management interface access with valid credentials now grants full root control of PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls; critical for network ops managing perimeter security and incident response.

CVE-2026-0322 affects PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls and Panorama (virtual and M-Series); Cloud NGFW and Prisma Access unaffected. Very limited exploitation observed with three IPs identified in advisory. Fixed releases available for PAN-OS 10.2, 11.1, 11.2, 12.1, 12.2 with 11.2.16 expected October 15. This continues 2026's pattern of firewall management interfaces as primary attack surface, compounding risk for teams still managing unpatched internet-facing PA deployments already exposed to CVE-2026-0257 (GlobalProtect auth bypass), which ransomware groups Qilin and Settra actively exploit.

NetClaw open-source network engineering agent presented at MCP summit

Agentic AI Foundation · Oct 14, 2026 · Primary source

John Capobianco from Itential presented NetClaw, an open-source network engineering agent, at MCP Dev Summit Toronto on October 6, 2026. NetClaw integrates pyATS, MCP, network APIs, observability, security, and documentation into a single agent organized around skills, tools, evidence, and constraints.

Why it matters NetClaw demonstrates a practitioner-led approach to agentic network operations: grounding agent reasoning in deterministic tools (Cisco pyATS), connecting to management systems via MCP, and enforcing operational constraints—a replicable pattern for teams operationalizing agents on real infrastructure.

John Capobianco from Itential developed NetClaw over 18 months after recognizing early that MCP was essential for networking operations. While Itential was connecting AI requests to workflows, validation, and approvals, he independently explored MCP for networking—matching the core question of how to make agents useful on real infrastructure. By February 2026, that became NetClaw, an open network engineering agent integrating pyATS (Cisco's gold-standard network testing framework), MCP, network APIs, observability platforms, security constraints, and documentation into a single open-source project. The agent is organized around skills, tools, evidence, and constraints—enabling it to reason about network state deterministically while respecting operational guardrails. This is a hands-on example of operationalizing agentic network operations: grounding agent reasoning in deterministic tools, connecting to management systems through standard protocols, and enforcing operational constraints. The project is publicly available for teams starting their own network agent implementations.

OpenTelemetry Collector stabilises Prometheus native histogram mapping

GitHub Releases · Oct 14, 2026 · Primary source

OpenTelemetry Collector v0.163.0 released October 14, 2026 with Prometheus exporter stabilizations including native histogram mapping and resource constant labels configuration. Hostmetrics receiver adds release-candidate semantic conventions metrics for process telemetry moving toward stable v1.0.

Why it matters Standardizes process-level observability metrics across platforms; critical for teams building portable monitoring pipelines that move beyond application-only instrumentation.

The v0.163.0 release continues OpenTelemetry's incremental standardization of telemetry collection. The addition of release-candidate semantic conventions for host metrics in the hostmetrics receiver marks progress for infrastructure observability within OTel's standards process. This brings parity to application, infrastructure, and business metrics under one specification framework. The Prometheus exporter compatibility layer sees three stabilizations: content negotiation, native histogram mapping, and resource constant labels configuration. No OTLP wire protocol changes or trace semantics shifts occurred, keeping this release low-risk for existing deployments. For platform teams managing collector fleets at scale, this represents incremental progress toward complete semantic convention coverage without breaking existing telemetry pipelines.

Most EMEA firms escalate AI incidents to the board

IBM · Oct 15, 2026 · Vendor release

AI-related incidents are reaching boardrooms across Europe and the Middle East, with 80% of executives reporting board or C-suite escalation in the past year, yet only 40% changed AI governance policy in response. 75% expect AI agents to have autonomy by 2027, but only 37% say governance is designed to scale with agentic AI deployment.

Why it matters Governance capabilities and risk accountability are not keeping pace with adoption, creating operational and regulatory risk for enterprises deploying AI at scale.

The survey found that 80% of EMEA executives experienced AI-related incidents reaching their board or C-suite in the past year, with only 40% changing AI governance policy or frameworks in response. This disconnect between incident frequency and governance response reflects a critical maturity gap. 75% of respondents expect AI agents to have some level of autonomy with respect to decisions by 2027, yet only 37% say their governance is designed to scale with agentic AI deployment. Only 37% of respondents have a formal, board-reviewed quantification of the financial risks created by gaps in their AI governance. The research highlights that enterprises are deploying autonomous AI faster than their governance frameworks can adapt, creating exposure to regulatory and operational risk. Supply chain visibility is also weak: 54% of respondents acknowledge gaps or limitations in how their governance framework covers AI systems and models used by key vendors and technology partners.
