First co-packaged optics switches ship in volume

InsideDeepTech · Nov 23, 2026 · Analysis

First production CPO switches are shipping: Broadcom Bailly 51.2T CPO Ethernet and NVIDIA Quantum-X Photonics Q3450-LD 144×800G InfiniBand deployed; TSMC Compact Universal Photonic Engine entering CoWoS-based production; LPO remains the 2026-2027 bridge technology for teams requiring field-replaceable modules.

Why it matters CPO production ramp directly reduces data center power consumption by 30% at cluster level by eliminating 3-4x copper-to-optical conversions per link; LPO remains the mainstream 2026-2027 choice for teams wanting field-replaceable modules while CPO qualification catches up.

Co-packaged optics (CPO) is now entering volume production with the first production CPO switches shipping from multiple vendors. Network power drops sharply with CPO: eliminating four to five copper-to-optical conversions per link cuts interconnect power 3–4x at the link level and ~30% at the cluster level—direct savings against the data center power crisis. The optical engine is co-packaged inside the switch ASIC package; a split pattern where the optical engine integrates with the switch while the laser remains a pluggable field-replaceable module has emerged as the 2026 production pattern. Linear pluggable optics (LPO) ships in volume today, cuts about 50% of the pluggable's DSP power, and requires no new packaging technology. Hyperscalers are buying LPO in 2025–2026 as a bridge while CPO ramps. For scale-out AI fabric deployments in 2026, 800G LPO or conventional pluggable remains the mainstream choice due to multi-vendor availability and operational familiarity. Broadcom Bailly (51.2T CPO), NVIDIA's Quantum-X Q3450-LD (144×800G InfiniBand with CPO roadmap), and TSMC COUPE on CoWoS-on-substrate are all entering volume production, while 1.6T CPO deployments remain gated behind hyperscaler qualification.

Chinese APT roots 25 Palo Alto firewalls via exploit chain

SecurityWeek / Cyber Security News · Nov 25, 2026 · Industry news

A Chinese-linked threat actor has compromised 25 Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS firewalls by chaining PHP2Shell RCE with Fragnesia (CVE-2026-46300) kernel LPE to achieve unauthenticated root access on internet-exposed management interfaces. Twenty-four devices were perimeter firewalls.

Why it matters Internet-exposed PAN-OS management interfaces are under active attack with no workaround; every organization must patch all five supported PAN-OS branches immediately to prevent root compromise of perimeter security infrastructure.

On November 25, Palo Alto Networks confirmed active exploitation of CVE-2026-46300 (Fragnesia), a Linux kernel local privilege-escalation flaw in PAN-OS, chained with CVE-2026-109618 (PHP2Shell) pre-auth RCE to achieve unauthenticated root access. All observed attacks targeted internet-exposed management interfaces; the attack chain required no authentication. The initial entry point is PHP2Shell on the management interface, followed by Fragnesia to escalate from limited privileges to root. Twenty-five firewalls were compromised, 24 positioned on network perimeters, with one management server also breached. This represents root-level compromise of critical perimeter security infrastructure. Palo Alto published advisory PAN-SA-2026-0015 on November 25, releasing patches across all five actively supported PAN-OS branches (10.2, 11.1, 11.2, 12.1, 12.2). Federal agencies and all organizations with internet-exposed PAN-OS management interfaces must upgrade immediately; no workaround mitigates this. Organizations that restrict management access to trusted internal addresses only face significantly lower risk and can upgrade at next scheduled maintenance.

OpenSSL patches critical heap overflow and QUIC memory exhaustion

OpenSSL / CVE Brief · Nov 24, 2026 · Primary source

OpenSSL published two critical vulnerabilities on November 24: CVE-2026-121481 (CVSS 9.8) is a heap buffer overflow in EVP_EncodeBlock() due to integer overflow on large inputs; CVE-2026-84785 (CVSS 9.1) enables memory exhaustion via unbounded QUIC stream fragment reassembly.

Why it matters Both vulnerabilities affect OpenSSL embedded across TLS terminators, appliances, and applications; patch assessment and deployment planning required for base64 encoding and QUIC transport services.

CVE-2026-121481 (CVSS 9.8) is a heap buffer overflow in EVP_EncodeBlock(), the base64 encoding function. For very large inputs approaching INT_MAX, the function's calculation of encoded output length can exceed integer range, causing the encoder to write beyond the supplied buffer boundary, corrupting nearby memory or crashing applications. This affects any service that encodes large binary data via OpenSSL's EVP interface. CVE-2026-84785 (CVSS 9.1) affects OpenSSL's QUIC implementation: a remote attacker can trigger unbounded out-of-order fragment reassembly by sending many small stream fragments while withholding the first byte, forcing OpenSSL to buffer reassembly nodes and exhaust memory across tens of megabytes per stream, driving DoS across many connections. Both vulnerabilities require patching across affected versions. CVE-2026-84785 is fixed in 4.1.1, 4.0.4, and 3.5.10; CVE-2026-121481 affects multiple branches. Organizations should prioritize patching services handling untrusted input or exposed QUIC endpoints.

Cisco Expressway root flaw exploited through hard-coded Erlang cookie

CISA / SecurityWeek / Infosecurity Magazine · Nov 23, 2026 · Industry news

CISA added CVE-2026-76466 to Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 20 after F6 security firm reported active exploitation. Russian cybersecurity firm F6 documented compromises of four clustered Cisco Expressway X15.5.3 devices via public proof-of-concept code released November 5.

Why it matters Clustered Cisco Expressway systems are under active attack with unauthenticated root RCE; organizations must patch immediately to X15.5.20 or migrate to fixed releases; no workaround exists.

Cisco Expressway Series uses a cluster database service running on Erlang that relies on persistent hard-coded cookies for inter-node authentication (CVE-2026-76466, CVSS 9.8). An unauthenticated remote attacker with network access to TCP 4371–4372 can join the cluster, obtain the hard-coded Erlang cookie, and invoke remote procedure calls (RPC) to execute arbitrary commands as root. The flaw was fixed on October 21 in release X15.5.20, but only clustered Expressway-C and Expressway-E are affected; standalone (unclustered) deployments are not vulnerable. Public proof-of-concept code landed on GitHub November 5. F6 security firm reported the first observed intrusion on November 19: attackers compromised two internet-facing Expressway X15.5.3 devices and pivoted to two additional cluster members inside the network. CISA added the CVE to its KEV catalog November 20 with federal remediation due November 23, 2026. No workaround exists; Cisco recommends immediate upgrade to X15.5.20. Organizations running X14 or earlier have no patch on that branch and must migrate to fixed releases. For organizations unable to upgrade, restricting TCP 4371–4372 access at firewall layer to trusted cluster node IPs only provides temporary mitigation.

For SRE teams running agents in AWS, this eliminates a critical blocker: prior to this, agent action cost attribution was opaque, preventing finance-aware governance. The token tracing also satisfies compliance requirements for showing human accountability behind autonomous actions. These changes move agent infrastructure from experimental to production-accountable, though infrastructure teams still need to define which agent actions require pre-approval versus post-audit.

RFC 10057 standardises YANG model for traffic engineering tunnels

IETF RFC Editor · Nov 18, 2026 · Primary source

RFC 10057 defines a comprehensive YANG data model for provisioning and managing Traffic Engineering (TE) tunnels, Label Switched Paths (LSPs), and interfaces. This standardizes TE management across vendor implementations with common YANG types and device models.

Why it matters Provides operators a standard method to automate TE provisioning and management across heterogeneous equipment, reducing configuration variance and enabling programmatic control via NETCONF/RESTCONF.

RFC 10057 addresses a long-standing gap in standards-based TE management by providing complete YANG modules (ietf-te and ietf-te-device) aligned with the TEAS working group architecture. The model covers tunnel creation, LSP configuration, interface parameters, and resource constraints. Published alongside RFC 10056 (Common YANG Data Types for Traffic Engineering), it provides operators a unified interface for multi-vendor TE deployments. The revision date of 2026-11-18 indicates this is fresh standardization work. For NetDevOps practitioners, this enables infrastructure-as-code approaches for TE tunnels and closes a gap where many networks still rely on vendor-specific CLIs or proprietary automation. The model integrates with existing YANG standards (RFC 8345 network topologies, RFC 9911 common types) to fit into broader network automation stacks. Operators can now provision LSPs, configure tunnel constraints, and manage interfaces declaratively across equipment from multiple vendors using a single data model.

RFC 10094 clarifies SRv6 segment identifier list processing

IETF RFC Editor · Nov 18, 2026 · Primary source

RFC 10094 clarifies Segment Routing over IPv6 (SRv6) Segment Identifier (SID) List Processing, resolving ambiguities in how SRv6 implementations must interpret and process SID lists to address interoperability issues in multi-vendor deployments.

Why it matters Eliminates implementation ambiguities in SRv6 that cause interoperability failures, reducing troubleshooting burden and enabling more reliable segment routing deployments across vendor boundaries.

RFC 10094 provides clarification on a critical but underspecified aspect of SRv6: how segment identifiers in an SID list are processed by nodes along the path. Prior guidance left room for vendor interpretation differences, causing reachability and policy enforcement failures in multi-vendor networks. This clarification ensures deterministic behavior when packets traverse nodes from different vendors. For network operators running SRv6 for traffic engineering, segment protection, or SD-WAN use cases, this RFC closes a gap that has required workarounds or vendor-specific configuration tweaks. The timing of this publication suggests it addresses real-world deployment issues that have emerged as SRv6 adoption has increased. Operators relying on SRv6 for path computation and enforced traffic steering should reference this RFC when troubleshooting multi-vendor SID list processing anomalies. The standardized clarification reduces vendor-specific behavior and enables more predictable operations.

LLM-driven xApp turns natural language into 5G RAN configs

arXiv · Nov 19, 2026 · Research

LLM-driven xApp enables runtime deployment of traffic-control functions in programmable RANs by grounding free-form natural-language inputs in RAN configuration parameters. Approach uses LLMs to translate dynamic service intents into configurations without predefined action spaces.

Why it matters Demonstrates practical path for intent-based RAN management via LLMs; operationally significant for telco operators building self-adaptive 5G systems that respond to changing traffic patterns and business requirements without manual intervention.

This paper addresses a real operational problem in programmable 5G RANs: static traffic-control policies become stale as network conditions and service requirements change dynamically. Conventional approaches rely on manually crafted rules or ML-based controllers with fixed state/action spaces defined at design time. Authors propose an LLM-driven xApp that accepts natural-language descriptions of desired network behavior (e.g., "prioritize latency-sensitive video traffic") and generates appropriate PFCP and O-RAN configurations. The LLM acts as semantic interpreter between high-level business intent and low-level RAN parameters. For 5G operations teams, this represents a step toward autonomous RAN optimization that reduces manual tuning and adapts to business SLAs. The approach avoids hardcoding policy rules per use case and leverages LLMs' ability to map semantically similar intents to compatible configurations. Practical implications: operators can express policies in natural language, reducing training burden on operations teams and enabling faster response to new service types.
