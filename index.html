1.6T Ethernet NICs and co-packaged optics reach production

The Network DNA · Oct 16, 2026 · Analysis

IEEE 802.3dj is targeted for completion in 2026, with 1.6T-capable NICs shipping and hyperscaler deployments of 1.6T spine switches beginning in production; co-packaged optics (CPO) entering production at lead customers.

Why it matters Early 1.6T switches and optics began appearing in 2025; broader deployment expected in 2026 as IEEE 802.3dj finalizes and 1.6T-capable NICs ship, with volume adoption in AI data centers from 2026 to 2027. Network teams should begin qualification processes now.

IEEE 802.3dj targeted for completion in 2026, with 1.6T-capable NICs shipping and hyperscaler deployments of 1.6T spine switches beginning; CPO enters production at lead customers, and by 2027 volume ramps with LPO and CPO taking meaningful share. The architectural breakthrough is the 200 Gbps serial lane—instead of bonding sixteen 100G lanes, 1.6T runs 8 lanes of 200G each (8 x 200G), built on 224G SerDes and PAM4 signaling. IEEE P802.3dj introduces inter-sublayer link training (ILT)—a standardized, negotiated equalization and tuning handshake between sublayers during bring-up, mandatory for 200G-per-lane PHYs and attachment unit interfaces. For practitioners, the 2026 completion of IEEE 802.3dj represents the moment pluggable optical qualification becomes urgent; vendor interoperability testing should begin now against early-access 1.6T modules to de-risk 2026-2027 deployments.

Malicious bots shifted hosts within hours of Yandex outage

Cloudflare Blog · Oct 14, 2026 · Primary source

Following Ukrainian drone strikes on Yandex data centers (Oct 7–11), traffic from Yandex's two main ASNs collapsed 86–99%. Cloudflare's analysis shows Yandex Search traffic drop was legitimate search crawling, while malicious bot traffic from Yandex.Cloud migrated to alternative Russian hosting ASNs (Radist, Selectel) within hours, maintaining identical TLS fingerprints and request patterns.

Why it matters Demonstrates infrastructure resilience tactics for malicious automation and shows how ASN-level outages cause rapid traffic migration rather than cessation—critical for understanding BGP failover behavior and threat actor adaptability.

Cloudflare analyzed HTTP traffic patterns across two Yandex autonomous systems (AS13238 for search, AS200350 for cloud hosting) following reported physical damage to data centers. Yandex Search ASN lost 99% of crawler traffic, almost entirely legitimate YandexBot indexing; 93.6% of its traffic was verified search crawlers (page indexing, image rendering, news crawling). By contrast, Yandex.Cloud saw 40% decline in malicious bot traffic and 90% drop in WordPress vulnerability scanning, but Cloudflare observed the same automated clients—identifiable by matching JA4 TLS fingerprints, custom User-Agent strings, and HTTP request paths—reappear on competing Russian hosting ASNs like Radist and Selectel within hours. Total attack volumes targeting gambling and other verticals remained essentially flat week-over-week, indicating seamless workload migration. This illustrates how BGP routing and peering changes cascade through infrastructure, forcing operators to reroute traffic but not disrupting the underlying threat operations—a key operational lesson for network teams managing resilience.

DDoS config change cut Inngest data center links for 44 minutes

Inngest Blog · Oct 15, 2026 · Primary source

On October 9, a data center provider lost bidirectional network connectivity between data centers and to the internet; root cause was an error during a DDoS protection configuration change. Accounts in that facility could not reach the primary database, halting queue processing until connectivity returned 44 minutes later.

Why it matters Demonstrates how BGP-adjacent DDoS mitigation configuration errors cascade into multi-site outages; relevant for operators managing active-active architectures and understanding failure modes in upstream provider misconfigurations.

Inngest operates a distributed queue processor across multiple data center providers. On October 9 at 21:05 UTC, one provider's data center (unidentified) lost network connectivity in both directions: inter-DC links and outbound internet access went down simultaneously. The provider's preliminary explanation cited an error made during a configuration change to its DDoS protection system—likely a BGP prefix filter, route withdrawal, or traffic policy rule that inadvertently blocked their own traffic. Inngest's impact was immediate: accounts processed in that facility could not reach the primary database lookup service (in a different facility), so queue processing halted. Run state could not be persisted either. The outage lasted 44 minutes (21:05–21:49 UTC). Upon reconnection, queue processing resumed and backlogs drained automatically. A few internal services did not recover autonomously and required manual restart between 22:26–22:38 UTC. This is a textbook case of how upstream DDoS mitigation changes—often deployed via BGP route manipulation or egress filtering—can create hard stops in customer traffic if not validated before deployment.

Exploited PAN-OS root RCE added to CISA KEV catalog

SecurityWeek · Oct 15, 2026 · Industry news

Palo Alto Networks released patches for CVE-2026-0322, a PAN-OS remote code execution zero-day exploited in attacks. The flaw allows authenticated attackers with network access to execute arbitrary code with root privileges on firewall management interfaces. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on October 14 with an October 17 federal remediation deadline.

Why it matters Affects PA-Series, VM-Series firewalls and Panorama—your edge perimeter is actively under attack. Immediate patching of internet-facing appliances is critical for any organization running Palo Alto firewalls.

CVE-2026-0322 is a high-severity RCE in PAN-OS management interface (CVSS 8.6) allowing authenticated attackers with network access to execute arbitrary code with root privileges on targeted firewalls. Very limited exploitation has been observed in the wild. No special configuration is needed to exploit the vulnerability. It affects PAN-OS on PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls and Panorama (virtual and M-Series appliances); Cloud NGFW and Prisma Access are not affected. No complete workaround exists—Palo Alto advises restricting management interface access to trusted internal IP addresses through a hardened jump box. Fixed versions include PAN-OS 12.2.4, 12.1.10-h2, 11.2.16, 11.1.16-h3, and 10.2.18-h12. The early addition to CISA KEV reflects active exploitation and signals federal agencies must remediate by October 17. Organizations should prioritize patching internet-facing and edge-deployed appliances first.

Juniper patches critical Junos J-Web and routing daemon flaws

SecurityOnline · Oct 15, 2026 · Industry news

Juniper Networks published October 2026 security bulletins on October 14 resolving dozens of critical Junos OS vulnerabilities. CVE-2026-101983 (CVSS 9.8) is a stack buffer overflow in httpd allowing unauthenticated RCE via J-Web virtual domains. CVE-2026-101954 (CVSS 9.8) enables full device takeover when J-Web is enabled. Multiple additional 9.8 CVSS flaws allow malformed BGP or EVPN traffic to crash routing daemon or compromise Routing Engine.

Why it matters Multiple 9.8 CVSS flaws in core routing and web management interfaces demand immediate inventory and patching of internet-facing Juniper devices. Affects core and peering routers deployed by ISPs, telcos, and hyperscalers.

The combined Junos OS and Junos OS Evolved bulletin (JSA112187) addresses over 60 routing and management bugs spanning PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls, MX-Series, PTX-Series, and SRX-Series platforms. CVE-2026-101983 (CVSS 9.8) is a stack-based buffer overflow in httpd allowing unauthenticated network attackers to cause RCE and DoS via J-Web virtual domains. CVE-2026-101954 (CVSS 9.8) allows unauthenticated network access to trigger full device takeover when J-Web is enabled. CVE-2026-101990, CVE-2026-101982, CVE-2026-102178, and CVE-2026-33789 allow malformed BGP or EVPN traffic to crash the routing protocol daemon or compromise the Routing Engine. Attackers can exploit these flaws via network protocols, management interfaces, and local services. The combination of management-plane (J-Web) and routing-plane (BGP/EVPN) flaws creates both direct takeover risk and protocol-level denial-of-service vectors. Given Juniper's role in ISP, telco, and hyperscale cloud fabric deployments, these patches should be prioritized immediately for production routers and edge devices.

Researchers propose analog RF computing for edge AI inference

arXiv · Oct 14, 2026 · Research

Paper proposes using base stations to multicast weight-encoded RF waveforms enabling edge devices to perform neural network matrix-vector multiplications through passive RF mixers with extremely low power and memory. Studies joint beamforming for simultaneous AI inference and conventional data transmission in MU-MIMO systems.

Why it matters Enables telecom operators to provide AI inference services to edge devices without on-device compute capacity or cloud offloading latency. Shifts computational burden to physical layer and network infrastructure, changing how operators allocate spectrum and power.

The paper addresses AI inference for resource-constrained edge devices by using analog RF computing, where a base station multicasts a waveform encoding neural network weights, and each client multiplies the received weight-encoded waveform with its locally generated input-encoded waveform through a passive mixer in the RF frontend. The authors study a multi-user MIMO downlink scenario where a multicast AI flow must share spectrum, power, and spatial resources with unicast data flows, formulating a joint beamforming problem to maximize aggregate data rate subject to computing accuracy, client energy, and power constraints. This approach shifts the computational burden to the physical layer and network infrastructure rather than device processors, with implications for how telecom operators manage spectrum and power allocation. The work challenges traditional device-centric inference architecture and suggests infrastructure-centric alternatives that can be more energy-efficient, particularly for IoT deployments. Networks would need to support specialized physical-layer computations, not just data transmission.

IETF advances YANG model for Ethernet traffic engineering topology

IETF CCAMP Working Group · Oct 15, 2026 · Primary source

Latest revision of Ethernet TE topology YANG data model defines standardized definitions for Ethernet networks used as client-layer or transport networks. Version 12 submitted October 15, 2026, advancing toward IESG publication with November 2027 target.

Why it matters Enables programmatic network topology management and controller-to-device communication for large-scale Ethernet infrastructure automation without vendor-specific APIs.

This YANG data model describes Ethernet networks when used either as a client-layer network of an underlay transport network (e.g., Optical Transport Network) or as a transport network itself. The model standardizes how Ethernet topology information—links, nodes, ports, capabilities—is represented in a vendor-neutral way. This is fundamental for network automation tools to discover and configure carrier-grade Ethernet infrastructure. Practitioners using YANG-based network controllers (OpenDaylight, ONAP, etc.) require standardized Ethernet topology models; this draft fills that gap and should eventually replace vendor-specific topology models. The ongoing progression through IETF indicates active operator and vendor alignment on standardizing Ethernet service topology.
