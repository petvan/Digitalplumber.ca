Inngest traces outage to network partition and PgBouncer pool collapse

Inngest Blog · Oct 15, 2026 · Primary source

Inngest's production incident report documents a data-center provider network partition (October 9) and subsequent PgBouncer connection pool collapse (October 10), revealing cascading failures across distributed systems and shared event pipelines.

Why it matters Practitioners need concrete examples of how network partitions propagate failures through dependency graphs; this shows why topology awareness and correlation are essential for NOC incident triage.

On October 9 at 21:05 UTC, Inngest experienced a network partition at one data center provider, losing bidirectional connectivity to other data centers and outbound internet access. Accounts with run state stored in the affected data center could not create, load, or save state. More critically, accounts in other data centers using shared event-processing pipelines were also impacted because failed work from the disconnected accounts built a large backlog, delaying event processing for unaffected accounts. Service was restored at 21:49. On October 10, both PgBouncer connection poolers (running in AWS) simultaneously lost all connections to the primary database at 14:00 and again at 15:30, unable to rebuild connections. A mitigation route through NAT gateways introduced silent connection drops. This incident exemplifies why observability platforms must correlate network topology changes with application-layer failures: the NOC needed to distinguish between a network problem (partition), a database problem (connection pool exhaustion), and cascading secondary impacts on systems without direct dependencies on the affected infrastructure.

Why it matters CPO production enables escape from transceiver density and per-link power constraints; Tomahawk 6 Davisson and purpose-built systems provide operators options for scaling beyond 100k GPU deployments with 3.5x power efficiency improvement and simplified optics supply chains.

Co-packaged optics transitioned from lab validation to production deployment at OCP Global Summit 2026. Broadcom's Tomahawk 6–Davisson integrates 16 × 6.4-Tbps optical engines on-die, eliminating external transceiver cages and delivering material efficiency gains: channel loss drops from 22 dB (pluggable) to 4 dB, and per-link power falls from ~30 W to ~9 W. Celestica and Micas Networks each announced 1.6T systems using Davisson, with Micas offering a portfolio spanning 800G air-cooled (4RU, 128 × 800G ports), 1.6T liquid-cooled (2RU, 64 × 1.6T ports), and Davisson CPO (1RU, 64 MMC DR8 optical interfaces). NVIDIA Spectrum-X Photonics also entered production with early customer deployments. The shift addresses thermal and space constraints in hyperscale clusters: dense 1.6T port counts require either aggressive liquid cooling or CPO integration to remain operationally feasible. Pluggable 1.6T transceivers face supply bottlenecks and per-port cost penalties; CPO sidesteps both by pooling lasers and moving optics inside the chassis. Operators targeting 2027 deployments can now specify CPO architectures and begin power-budget planning. Traditional pluggable 800G remains production-ready for near-term builds through 2026–2027, providing a technology bridge.

RPKI now protects 70% of internet routes after China surge

IPinfo · Oct 15, 2026 · Analysis

RPKI coverage jumped from 54% to 70.3% of routed prefixes in one year, driven largely by China's adoption surge from 3.7% to 83%. Invalid routes barely propagate in practice, reaching only ~2 collectors vs ~596 for valid ones.

Why it matters Validates RPKI deployment momentum while exposing that 16.2% of covered IPv4 routes use loose maxLength settings, leaving room for attacks like the Hetzner hijack.

IPinfo's measurement shows RPKI-valid routing now covers 70.3% of global prefixes and 65% of IPv4 address space, up from 54% and 53.1% respectively. China drove most of the 16.4-point gain, jumping from 3.7% to 83% of its IPv4 addresses. Critically, the data reveals that invalid RPKI routes barely propagate: vantage-point analysis shows they reach only ~2 collectors on average versus ~596 for valid routes, meaning raw invalid-route percentages often exaggerate real-world impact. However, one significant gap remains: 16.2% of IPv4 and 20.9% of IPv6 RPKI-covered routes have permissive ROA configurations with no maxLength constraint, creating 'open windows' like the one exploited in the August 2026 Hetzner BGP hijack where an attacker announced a /24 inside a /16 ROA. Of unprotected space, ~60% is in the US, with about one in four addresses belonging to DoD (mostly unused space for monitoring). The report recommends operators: publish ROAs for all announced prefixes, leave maxLength unset to constrain specificity, and audit ROAs periodically as some operators (Saudi Telecom, SEACOM, Orange Maroc) have lost ROA records without noticing.

Attack chains typically involve initial VPN foothold followed by pivot to internal infrastructure, with observed lateral movement to virtualization management appliances like vCenter.

RIPE Atlas study finds IP geolocation providers often inconsistent

arXiv · Oct 19, 2026 · Research

Longitudinal study (January–June 2026) comparing RIPE Atlas RTT measurements against IP geolocation provider location claims for 5.6M+ unique IPs using multilateration distance constraints. Finds consistency variance across four major providers (IPinfo, DB-IP, MaxMind, IP2Location) and distinct degradation patterns by IP type and address family.

Why it matters NetOps teams using IP geolocation for traffic steering, DDoS mitigation, or compliance must validate provider consistency empirically against measured RTT constraints rather than assuming provider accuracy across all address ranges.

Paper arXiv:2610.27318 (submitted 19 Oct 2026) presents the first large-scale empirical validation of geolocation providers using RIPE Atlas's global probe network. Methodology: analyzed 2.6+ billion measurement results from January–June 2026, testing whether geoprovider location estimates violate speed-of-light distance constraints given measured RTT values. Key findings: consistency levels remain mostly stable longitudinally but show distinct provider-specific degradation patterns. IPinfo leads DB-IP by 5.4 percentage points for IPv4 and 11.5 percentage points for IPv6 consistency rates. Different providers degrade differently on distinct IP address subsets (mobile vs. backbone, IPv4 vs. IPv6, routers vs. endpoints). For operations, the practical implication is clear: geolocation provider accuracy is not uniform. If you rely on geolocation for steering or analytics, run your own RIPE Atlas measurements to profile your chosen provider's behavior in your specific topology. The paper provides methodology and benchmarks for doing so at scale.
