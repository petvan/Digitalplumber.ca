Itential maps shift from static runbooks to agent-driven network ops

Itential blog · Oct 15, 2026 · Primary source

Itential published a practitioner-focused guide on deploying AI across network and infrastructure, covering the shift from static runbooks to agent-driven operations with human-in-the-loop controls and intent-first network design.

Why it matters Shows the practical operationalization of AI agents in network automation—from experimental assistants to autonomous, governed operations—directly applicable to AIOps and NetDevOps practice.

Joksan Flores (Itential Principal Solutions Engineer, formerly Cisco networking architect) outlines the 2026 reality of AI in network operations. Three key themes: (1) Agent-Driven Operations—agents replace static runbooks by negotiating, validating, and escalating intelligently; engineers supervise, refine intent models, and approve high-impact changes. (2) Intent-First Networking—engineers define outcomes (performance, security, resilience), and agent-driven systems determine execution across vendors, domains, and layers. (3) The missing pieces are now available: MCP (Model Context Protocol), modern AI agents, A2A protocols, and orchestration platforms. For teams building NetDevOps workflows, this is the roadmap: start with high-fidelity intent models (Nautobot Jobs, Itential FlowAgent Builder), wire in human gates (especially for production), and let agents handle the cross-domain orchestration. The post specifically addresses the 2026 gap: AI agents work well in labs, but production requires fail-safe design—rollback capabilities, human checkpoints, and structured intent validation. Updated October 15, 2026.

IP Infusion OcNOS adds Broadcom Tomahawk 6 at 102.4 terabits

Financial Content / IP Infusion · Oct 14, 2026 · Vendor release

IP Infusion announced OcNOS-DC support for Broadcom Tomahawk 6, enabling 102.4 Tbps fabric with 512 ports of 200G, supporting scale-up clusters of 128,000 XPUs in a two-layer leaf-spine topology.

Why it matters Eliminates super-spine layers and reduces hop count, cutting latency and optics cost while maintaining lossless RoCEv2 at full line rate for AI workloads.

OcNOS-DC on Tomahawk 6 delivers native 1.6T ports supporting scale-up networks connecting 512 accelerators in a single hop, while scale-out clusters reach 128,000 XPUs in a two-layer leaf-spine design. A flatter fabric topology reduces hops at every scale: for scale-up, twice the capacity in every switch means fewer devices in the traffic path; for scale-out, eliminating the super-spine layer dramatically lowers latency. OcNOS-DC provides Priority Flow Control (PFC) and Explicit Congestion Notification (ECN) to maintain lossless RoCEv2 transport at full line rate. With 512×200G ports, operators can connect more accelerators per leaf and build larger clusters with fewer switches. Scale-across topologies benefit from 1.6T and 800G uplinks feeding interconnect platforms running OcNOS, keeping data-center-to-data-center capacity in sync with growing intra-cluster demands.

New RFC explains how to configure ECN on UDP sockets

RFC Editor / IETF · Oct 14, 2026 · Primary source

RFC 10055 provides guidance on configuring UDP sockets for Explicit Congestion Notification (ECN) across common platforms, published as an informational RFC in October 2026. The document addresses practical implementation considerations for ECN deployment in UDP-based applications.

Why it matters Practitioners deploying ECN for congestion management in real-time and delay-sensitive UDP applications need portable, cross-platform socket configuration patterns; this RFC codifies best practices across Linux, Windows, and BSD systems.

RFC 10055, authored by M. Duke, standardizes ECN socket configuration patterns across operating systems. ECN is increasingly critical for modern networking infrastructure, particularly in data center and WAN scenarios where congestion signals enable traffic management without packet loss. However, implementation varies significantly across platforms and network stacks. This RFC reduces deployment friction and improves interoperability. For network operators deploying congestion-aware applications and AIOps systems relying on fast feedback signals, consistent ECN implementation improves observability of network state and enables better demand-responsive traffic engineering.
