Oracle adds multiplanar failover to Acceleron AI networking fabric

Oracle · Oct 27, 2026 · Primary source

Oracle announced new capabilities in Oracle Acceleron, OCI's suite of networking and infrastructure technologies for running demanding AI workloads reliably, securely, and at scale. Features include a Collective Communications Library for handling network path failures and Transparent Front-End Multiplanar Networking with built-in failover and congestion control for front-end RDMA traffic.

Why it matters OCI's programmable networking architecture with native multiplanar support and transparent RDMA control provides operational model alternative to NVIDIA Spectrum-X for large-scale AI cloud operators and sovereign AI builders.

OCI is pursuing a multiyear buildout of AI data centers with individual projects requiring hundreds of megawatts, having reported adding 850 MW of data center capacity and deploying more than 300,000 GPUs during fiscal first quarter 2027. Oracle Acceleron combines dedicated network fabrics, programmable networking via AMD Pensando DPUs, and direct data paths. AMD Pensando DPUs power Oracle Acceleron SmartNICs, with the companies co-engineering this foundation to move networking, storage, security and infrastructure functions closer to the data path, preserving host CPU capacity for customer applications. The Collective Communications Library helps customers keep distributed AI workloads running when network paths fail by spreading collective traffic across OCI's backend Multiplanar Network. Transparent front-end multiplanar support brings multi-path resilience to customer-facing networks, reducing tail latency impact when a plane fails. Focus on distributed AI suggests OCI targeting multi-rack, multi-pod cluster scales. Part of October Oracle AI World announcements including OpenAI and Google TPU integrations.

BMP monitoring can catch MAC hijacks in EVPN fabrics

APNIC Blog · Oct 28, 2026 · Industry news

EVPN MAC mobility enables seamless workload movement, but MAC spoofing can amplify damage by redirecting traffic through BGP. Operators can export BGP updates using BMP to a collector for route-reflector monitoring and detect anomalous MAC moves.

Why it matters Fabric operators using EVPN need visibility into MAC mobility events and BGP path anomalies to prevent hijacks in data center environments.

Live VM migration moves running workloads between hypervisors while retaining MAC addresses. When it arrives on the new host, it sends a gratuitous ARP message, allowing the new leaf to learn the MAC locally before BGP updates redirect traffic with a single update, allowing the VM to maintain active sessions. With EVPN, MAC learning moves into the control plane. The MAC Mobility Extended Community is a transitive BGP Extended Community carried within Type-2 routes. Every PE that imports the EVPN instance's Route Target through route reflectors will propagate the update. The risk is that a forged MAC address can redirect traffic across the fabric through BGP. Operators can export routing updates using BGP Monitoring Protocol (BMP) to a collector to gain visibility into these control-plane changes and detect anomalous MAC moves before they impact production workloads. This is especially critical as EVPN deployments scale in modern data centers where rapid workload migration is routine.

Forged documents let attacker hijack LACNIC routes via transit provider

btw.media · Oct 25, 2026 · Industry news

APNIC and LACNIC investigated three brief route hijacks of LACNIC address space in July 2025. An attacker used forged letterhead and a lookalike domain to convince a multinational transit provider to enable BGP for an unauthorized ASN, then announced spoofed routes.

Why it matters Operators must implement strict ASN onboarding controls and BGP session validation—forged corporate identity can bypass technical controls at ingress.

In July 2025, LACNIC received a complaint that led investigators to three brief BGP hijacks of its address space, lasting about 20, 15 and 5 minutes respectively. The attacker used forged letterhead and a deceptively similar domain to persuade a multinational transit provider to turn up a BGP connection to an unauthorized ASN. Once inside, the attacker used the stolen AS as transit and announced short-lived routes from other spoofed origin ASNs. The genuine holder reported the fraudulent session to the transit provider, ending the session. The key operational lesson: the attacker used social engineering, forged identity documents and a lookalike domain to convince an upstream provider to enable BGP for an unauthorized ASN, which is why onboarding belongs in the routing playbook. The public records do not measure diverted traffic, but the demonstrated failure is the opening of an unauthorized path. A route can look technically plausible after a provider has accepted a forged corporate identity.

Attackers exploit PHP2Shell flaws on Palo Alto management interfaces

Palo Alto Networks, CISA, Hacker News · Oct 29, 2026 · Primary source

Threat actors are actively exploiting PHP2Shell vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618, CVE-2026-109619) against publicly-accessible Palo Alto Networks management interfaces; unauthenticated remote code execution affects all PHP installations including Apache mod_php, PHP-FPM, php-cgi, FrankenPHP and LiteSpeed.

Why it matters CISA added CVE-2026-46300 to KEV catalog requiring federal agencies to patch by October 31, 2026; any internet-exposed PAN-OS management interface requires immediate patching as exploit is actively chained with kernel privilege-escalation bugs for full system compromise.

Active exploitation has been observed targeting Palo Alto Networks management interfaces exposed to the internet. Palo Alto Networks confirmed that PAN-OS and Panorama are affected by PHP2Shell. Unit 42 reports the vulnerability being used against internet-exposed PAN-OS management interfaces, chained with Linux kernel privilege-escalation bugs to reach root. As of October 30, 2026, patches have been released for all vulnerable versions of PAN-OS 12.2, 12.1, 11.2, 11.1, and 10.2. Fortinet rated CVE-2026-109618 critical with CVSS 9.8; Cisco lists vulnerabilities critical; Palo Alto rates the issue high with CVSS-B score 8.7. Infrastructure teams managing Palo Alto firewalls must immediately apply patches and restrict management interface access to specific networks.

Exploited Cisco firewall VPN flaw reloads ASA and FTD devices

Cisco, CISA, OpenVPN · Oct 28, 2026 · Primary source

Cisco disclosed CVE-2026-76418, an actively exploited unauthenticated remote denial-of-service flaw in Secure Firewall ASA and FTD Remote Access SSL VPN service (CVSS 8.6) that triggers unexpected device reloads via crafted requests sent without requiring VPN authentication.

Why it matters CISA added CVE-2026-76418 to KEV on October 29; this vulnerability directly disrupts VPN availability for remote workers and branch sites, requiring immediate identification of affected ASA/FTD releases and patching or segmentation controls.

Cisco published the security advisory on October 28, 2026, after learning of active exploitation during October. The flaw stems from logic errors in the Remote Access SSL VPN service when secondary authentication is configured on a tunnel group. A successful VPN login is not required to reach the affected processing path. This represents the second Cisco firewall VPN zero-day since August 2026. The High-severity (CVSS 8.6) unauthenticated remote denial-of-service vulnerability is caused by logic errors when secondary authentication is configured on a tunnel group. Network teams must check Cisco's advisory for affected versions and immediately apply patches or apply network segmentation controls restricting VPN service access if patching is delayed.

GoAhead TLS silently accepts revoked client certificates

Hacker News · Oct 27, 2026 · Industry news

GoAhead TLS library version 6.1.0 and earlier silently accepts revoked client certificates when using OpenSSL backend with mutual-TLS and revocation lists configured; CVE-2026-115881 (CVSS 9.1) allows attackers holding revoked keys to authenticate without revocation enforcement.

Why it matters Teams using GoAhead or similar embedded TLS libraries for mTLS authentication must immediately verify revocation list enforcement is active and upgrade to version 6.1.1; revoked certificates bypass zero-trust mechanisms until next revocation list refresh.

The OpenSSL backend silently accepts revoked client certificates despite configured revocation lists in GoAhead builds 6.1.0 and earlier; revocation list is loaded but never enforced. To be exploited, the build must use OpenSSL backend with client certificates required, trusted CA bundle and issuer checking enabled, and revocation list configured, with attacker holding a certificate signed by trusted authority and listed as revoked. Upgrade to 6.1.1, or enable issuer checking with trusted CA bundle, or use OpenSSL or LibreSSL 4.x. This is particularly critical for infrastructure using mTLS for device authentication, API gateways, and zero-trust implementations where certificate revocation is supposed to block compromised or departed client identities. Silent acceptance of revoked certificates means an attacker holding revoked keys could authenticate indefinitely until the next revocation list refresh.

Why it matters Demonstrates production-ready agentic NetOps workflow with explicit human-in-the-loop control, showing how enterprises can scale operations without proportional headcount growth.

Verizon says AI agents will push uplink ratios to 1:1

RCR Wireless News · Oct 27, 2026 · Industry news

Verizon CTO Santiago Tenorio outlined a shift from "transactional" AI to constant "context-aware" AI feeds, with uplink ratio possibly shifting 9:1 to 1:1 to support AI agent demands. With AI, agents will require as many video feeds and resolution as required depending on use case.

Why it matters 6G will be AI native, and the openness of interfaces and native standardization following Open RAN specification will accelerate AI inclusion into network design, requiring fundamental rearchitecture for uplink capacity and telemetry.

Verizon's CTO Yago Tenorio presented at the 6G Forum a critical architectural insight: AI workloads fundamentally reshape uplink requirements. Current networks assume asymmetric downlink-heavy traffic (roughly 9:1 downlink to uplink), reflecting consumer download patterns. AI agents operating across networks, robotics, and autonomous systems reverse this model. Tenorio frames it as a transition from "transactional" AI (prompt → answer) to "context-aware" AI requiring continuous sensor feeds, video streams, and high-resolution data. This demands symmetric or uplink-heavy capacity—potentially reaching 1:1 ratios. The architectural implications are material: network design must account for agents consuming arbitrary amounts of video, sensor data, and telemetry feeds depending on use case. Verizon references its work on Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) and AI wearables as bottom-up reference architectures. The message to infrastructure planners: 6G cannot be an incremental 5G upgrade focused on peak rate or latency alone. It must be designed AI-native from the start, with Open RAN's standardized, deterministic interfaces as the foundation. This shapes everything from spectrum allocation to fiber backhaul capacity planning.

Mistral opens weights for trillion-parameter security-focused model

TechCrunch · Oct 29, 2026 · Industry news

Mistral released the weights for its 1.05-trillion-parameter open-source model Mistral Large 4, allowing businesses and governments to download and deploy the system themselves. The model uses 47 percent fewer tokens per task than open-weight competitor GLM-5.3 and achieved 48% on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index.

Why it matters Organizations gain control over AI for defensive security operations with access that cannot be cut off because the model is open-weighted, eliminating reliance on API-dependent closed models for sensitive security work.

Mistral Large 4 is a 1-trillion-parameter multimodal model trained on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Europe, with 49 billion activated parameters and 52 billion including embedding and output layers. Mistral states this released version was optimized following real-world testing with government and enterprise users. Available under a modified MIT license supporting personal, academic, and commercial use. Large 4 ranks in the top five on the Artificial Analysis Cyber Index and leads open-weight models built outside China; on vulnerability reproduction and patching tasks, Large 4 scored 82%. The weights are available on Hugging Face and via Mistral's API. For practitioners, this represents a milestone: a 1T-parameter model capable of defensive cybersecurity work is now self-hostable, enabling organizations with EU data residency or airgap requirements to avoid cloud-dependent alternatives.
