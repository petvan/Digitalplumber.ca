Broadcom pitches disaggregated AI Ethernet fabric against Cisco Silicon One

TechBlog ComsOC · Nov 16, 2026 · Analysis

Broadcom presented its disaggregated AI fabric strategy at SC26 combining Tomahawk 6 (102.4 Tbps), Tomahawk Ultra (low-latency intra-rack), Jericho 4 (deep-buffer routing), and Thor Ultra 800G NIC with UEC compliance, contrasting with Cisco's unified Silicon One approach.

Why it matters Network architects must choose between Broadcom's specialized role-based ASICs (optimized per function) versus Cisco's unified programmable silicon; Broadcom's approach reduces switching latency and power per function, critical for 100k+ GPU clusters.

Broadcom demonstrated a disaggregated ASIC strategy specifically designed for massive AI cluster networking at SC26 on November 16. The strategy pairs four specialized silicon components: Tomahawk 6 (102.4 Tbps leaf-spine fabric switch with shallow buffers and high radix for scale-out), Tomahawk Ultra (51.2 Tbps with ~250ns latency and credit-based flow control for intra-rack GPU memory scale-up), Jericho 4 (deep-buffer scale-across spine router with HBM for congestion recovery), and Thor Ultra (800G NIC with full UEC compliance and advanced RoCE). This contrasts with Cisco's unified, P4-programmable Silicon One architecture (G300/G302 series offering 102.4 Tbps with shared packet buffer and path-based load balancing). Broadcom's approach optimizes silicon area and power efficiency by role: Tomahawk 6 keeps switch pipelines lean with shallow buffers for high-radix leaf-spine fabrics, offloading deep buffering to Jericho 4 and congestion recovery to Thor Ultra endpoints. The strategy combines packet-level multipathing, selective retransmission, and programmable congestion control. Network operators choosing between these architectures must weigh Broadcom's lower latency and power per function against Cisco's operational simplicity from unified management and P4 programmability across roles.

Arista EOS flaws let attackers bypass RPKI validation

Arista · Nov 10, 2026 · Vendor release

Arista released four security advisories on November 10, 2026, covering vulnerabilities in EOS's RPKI client and RIPv2 authentication. The RPKI vulnerabilities allow attackers controlling the cache or router-to-cache traffic to send crafted messages causing incorrect validation results or RPKI agent reset.

Why it matters These CVEs expose operational risks for networks relying on RPKI validation and RIPv2 authentication; adjacent-network attackers can interrupt routing services or inject unauthorized routes, directly undermining BGP security posture and RPKI ROV deployment confidence.

Arista security advisories 0226, 0223, and 0222 (all dated November 10, 2026) describe vulnerabilities in EOS's Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI) client, which obtains Route Origin Authorization (ROA) data from an external cache for BGP route validation. An attacker controlling the RPKI cache or router-to-cache traffic can send a crafted RPKI message that causes incorrect validation results or resets the RPKI agent. Advisory 0221 covers two vulnerabilities in RIPv2 MD5 authentication on Arista EOS-based products, whereby an unauthenticated attacker with adjacent network access may interrupt routing services or inject unauthorized routes. Advisory 0220 warns that on affected EOS platforms with RIPv2 configured, RIPv2 might be silently enabled on management interfaces even when disabled, allowing attackers with adjacent network access to send crafted RIPv2 route advertisements to management IP addresses that devices accept and install without authentication. These issues are critical for operators running RPKI ROV validation and present lateral attack surfaces on out-of-band management networks.

Route leak hit Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 despite valid ROA

LogicMonitor · Nov 13, 2026 · Analysis

On November 13–14 UTC, a route leak/hijack of Cloudflare's 1.1.1.0/24 DNS resolver prefix degraded Canadian resolver reachability. Despite Cloudflare holding a valid RPKI ROA for the prefix, the hijacked route originated by AS4755 was marked RPKI Invalid but still propagated and was accepted by networks including AS6453.

Why it matters This incident demonstrates that RPKI ROV validation alone is insufficient—networks must actively implement RPKI filtering on received routes; many operators accept RPKI-invalid announcements, undermining the security model and requiring defense-in-depth with prefix filtering and monitoring.

During November 13–14, 2026, a route leak affecting Cloudflare's 1.1.1.0/24 DNS prefix degraded resolver reachability in Canada. Cloudflare held a valid RPKI ROA for the prefix, authorizing only AS13335 (Cloudflare) to originate it. The hijacked route originated by AS4755 was clearly marked RPKI Invalid in RouteViews collectors. However, despite this validation failure, the announcements were accepted and propagated by networks including AS6453, and many nodes withdrew the legitimate route via AS13335. Some ISPs like Lumen and AT&T had no routes for 1.1.1.0/24 at all, suggesting they honored RPKI filtering and dropped invalid announcements. Tata itself is known to apply RPKI-based filtering, yet its own RPKI filtering did not catch this announcement. This incident exposes a critical gap: RPKI ROV validation works technically, but most networks do not implement mandatory RPKI filtering on received routes, allowing invalid announcements to propagate and disrupt services even when ROAs are properly configured.

Windows networking bug caused 24-hour Microsoft 365 outage

Microsoft Status Page / Multiple postmortems · Nov 13, 2026 · Primary source

A hidden Windows bug overwhelmed servers handling cloud connections across Microsoft 365, lasting from 4:20 PM UTC on November 12 until 4:10 PM UTC on November 13. Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, and Copilot were affected globally, with over 80% of affected gateway servers stopping responding at peak.

Why it matters Around 430 million Microsoft 365 users across 70+ countries experienced service loss; demonstrates how cascading failover in converged infrastructure amplifies failures and requires network teams to audit automatic health-check and traffic-rerouting logic.

The outage was caused by servers unexpectedly failing automated health checks and dropping out of service, which pushed more load onto remaining servers until they were overwhelmed. Users began losing Outlook access at 16:17 UTC on November 12, with 18 other services including Teams, Copilot and Intune also affected. Microsoft paused deployments, moved traffic between regions, restarted servers, tried alternative health checks, and reverted a recent network change. The root cause was a latent Windows defect on networking gateway servers triggered by a traffic shift and reduced capacity from an earlier network upgrade. This event illustrates a critical infrastructure risk: automated failover systems can amplify failures when triggered by systemic connectivity issues. Network teams running similar converged cloud platforms must review whether health-check logic can enter feedback loops and whether traffic rerouting has capacity buffers.

FRRouting bgpd crashes on dynamic neighbour disconnects

CVE.org / FRRouting Project · Nov 13, 2026 · Primary source

Double free in FRRouting bgpd (CVSS 8.2 High) when dynamic BGP neighbors disconnect causes denial of service. When a peer-group used by 'bgp listen range' has Site-of-Origin configured, dynamic neighbors share the SoO value but each frees it on disconnect, causing double-free and bgpd abort.

Why it matters Any external host within the listen range can trigger total BGP session loss by opening and closing two TCP connections to the BGP port without authentication; core routing infrastructure operators must patch FRRouting immediately or reconfigure to avoid listen ranges with Site-of-Origin values.

Each dynamic neighbor shares the peer-group's SoO value instead of getting its own copy, but frees it as its own when deleted; the first disconnect leaves the peer-group pointing at freed memory, and the second frees it again, causing bgpd to abort and drop all BGP sessions. Any host whose address falls inside the listen range can trigger this by opening and closing two TCP connections without sending any BGP message and without knowing the AS number, and can repeat it each time bgpd restarts. If the peer-group has TCP-MD5 password, only password-knowing hosts can trigger it. Between the two frees, displaying configuration or establishing a dynamic neighbor also reads freed memory, which can crash bgpd after a single disconnect. Only configurations combining listen range with SoO on the same peer-group are affected. Affects FRRouting 8.4 and later with no public exploit yet, but exploitation requires no authentication and can be triggered by any external host with BGP port access.

Isolated telco AI agents make conflicting network decisions

RCR Wireless News · Nov 12, 2026 · Analysis

Telecom operators have deployed AI agents across network operations, customer care and planning, but isolated systems cause conflicting decisions—network operations agents reroute traffic while unaware customer care agents issue credits and book technicians. A shared data layer providing unified view of customers, services and networks is required.

Why it matters NetOps and AIOps practitioners must address orchestration across isolated AI agents—shared data layers and unified event correlation are becoming architectural requirements for production automation at scale.

This practitioner perspective highlights an emerging operational maturity problem: operators have shipped AI agents into production in network operations, customer care, and capacity planning, but without coordination mechanisms. A concrete example illustrates the risk: in a metro fiber cut, the network operations agent reroutes traffic within seconds and reduces latency impact. Simultaneously, the customer care agent, unaware the issue has been mitigated, issues automatic service credits and books technician visits—incurring unnecessary cost and customer confusion. The architectural lesson: telcos maintain separate management systems for inventory, customer records, and planning tools, each representing network resources differently. AI agents see those representations as separate problems. The proposed solution is a shared data layer that reconciles those records and maps how network resources connect to services and customers—allowing agents to maintain domain specialization while operating on a unified view. The practitioner cites a Malaysian wireless operator that ingested events, metrics and topology from 20+ element management systems into a single assurance view, enabling agents to correlate network events to affected customers. For infrastructure teams, the implication is clear: modern telco automation requires treating data unification as a prerequisite to agent deployment, not a post-deployment integration task.
