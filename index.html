Lambda runs co-packaged optics across 10,000 Blackwell Ultra GPUs

HPCwire · Nov 16, 2026 · Primary source

Lambda announced the world's first large-scale deployment of co-packaged optics (CPO) networking connecting 10,000+ NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs using NVIDIA Photonics switches at its Quebec campus, demonstrating lower latency and improved signal stability at scale.

Why it matters CPO reduced network latency 3.8% at rack scale and 10.1% at pod scale versus pluggable modules while maintaining throughput, validating CPO for production AI clusters and reducing thermal load—directly improving GPU utilization and ROI per facility.

CPO places optical engines alongside the switch ASIC, shortening the electrical path from inches to millimeters, reducing signal loss, eliminating retimer chips, and increasing network resiliency. Each switch has 144 ports at 800 Gb/s with liquid cooling and 18 removable external light sources; Lambda's benchmarks showed median optical temperature of 36°C against 51°C for pluggables. This is the first published operator benchmark of co-packaged optics at cluster scale, with removable lasers enabling field-swappable failed sources rather than full switch replacement. Lambda's Quebec data center has 142 MW of power capacity and runs on nearly 100% renewable energy. This deployment directly addresses the power and thermal constraints that become critical at 1.6T and 3.2T speeds, shifting operational focus from optics density to reliability and thermal efficiency at scale.

Gateway defect and lost capacity caused 24-hour Microsoft 365 outage

DEV Community · Nov 19, 2026 · Analysis

Microsoft's preliminary post-incident report identified three factors: a latent defect in a July 2026 update to Microsoft 365 network gateway servers, extra load after Teams traffic shifted onto the Exchange Online gateway, and almost 14% of gateway capacity already offline. The outage affected 430+ million users across 70+ countries for approximately 24 hours.

Why it matters Operations engineers must implement canary deployments, circuit breakers on BGP route changes, and geographic failover testing; demonstrates cascade risk when traffic engineering assumptions change.

Microsoft identified three contributing factors: a software bug in Microsoft 365 networking gateway servers introduced in a July update; Teams failing over onto the same gateway infrastructure about 15 hours before the incident; and nearly 14% of gateway capacity already offline for unrelated reasons. The combination created a cascade—load concentration on already-degraded capacity with an undetected July bug. This was not a BGP hijack or cyber incident; the FAA Administrator stated it was not cyber-related, and Microsoft confirmed their incident was not cyber-related. The postmortem highlights the risk of treating network upgrades as low-impact when they alter traffic engineering assumptions: SDN policy distribution delays combined with BGP route metric conflicts created a convergence failure. This emphasizes the need for orchestrated rollouts, automated health checks between Teams and Exchange infrastructure, and reservation of unplanned capacity buffers.

Critical OpenSSL base64 overflow corrupts memory on large inputs

cybersecuritynews.com · Nov 20, 2026 · Industry news

A critical heap buffer overflow (CVSS 9.8) in OpenSSL's EVP_EncodeBlock() Base64 function can corrupt application memory when processing very large inputs; calculation of encoded length exceeds integer range causing buffer overflow.

Why it matters While CVSS 9.8 describes potential severity, not every OpenSSL-using service is remotely exposed; teams must audit where applications call this function and what size limits apply before encoding.

CVE-2026-121481, published November 18, 2026, tracks a heap buffer overflow in OpenSSL's Base64 encoding function with CVSS 3.1 score of 9.8. The bug affects EVP_EncodeBlock(), which converts binary data into printable text; for very large inputs, OpenSSL's calculation of the encoded length can exceed the range of the integer used to store it, causing the encoder to write beyond the buffer end and corrupt nearby memory or crash the application. Affects OpenSSL 4.0.0 through 4.0.3 and 4.1.0, with 4.0.4 and 4.1.1 as fixed versions. As of November 18, 2026, OpenSSL recorded 26 security vulnerabilities in the last 90 days with 15 rated High or Critical, though none are actively exploited per CISA's catalog. Teams should identify applications calling this function and determine applicable size limits before encoding large data.

OpenTelemetry-Go attribute length limit bypassed by Unicode characters

CVE / Open Telemetry Security Advisory · Nov 13, 2026 · Primary source

CVE-2026-128085 affects OpenTelemetry-Go versions 0.9.0–1.46.x: AttributeValueLengthLimit is bypassed by attribute values composed of valid U+FFFD replacement characters. Organizations must upgrade to 1.47.0 or later.

Why it matters Vulnerability allows remote attackers to bypass configured attribute size limits causing DoS through excessive memory consumption—critical for multi-tenant observability platforms where untrusted agents submit telemetry data.

The OpenTelemetry-Go SDK truncates attribute values longer than configured AttributeValueLengthLimit, but its counting mechanism skips valid U+FFFD replacement characters. The truncation mechanisms fail to correctly count U+FFFD characters when determining whether a value exceeds the configured limit, allowing values composed of these Unicode characters to bypass length restrictions entirely and exceed both character count and memory bounds for span attributes, log record fields, and auto-instrumentation trace data. This is an incomplete fix of an earlier truncation advisory (CVE-2026-81869) affecting SDK versions 1.33.0 to 1.46.x. The flaw presents a resource exhaustion vector in multi-tenant observability platforms. For infrastructure operators, immediate action includes auditing OpenConfig implementations (lemming, kne) and network telemetry exporters using otel/sdk/log prior to 1.47.0, then patching to the fixed version.

BGP FlowSpec steering into SRv6 policies clears IETF last call

IETF Datatracker · Nov 13, 2026 · Primary source

draft-ietf-idr-ts-flowspec-srv6-policy-22 (updated Nov 10, 2026) completes Working Group Last Call for BGP FlowSpec Extensions for SRv6 Policy as a Proposed Standard. Specifies procedures to steer traffic flows into Segment Routing Policies using BGP.

Why it matters Standardizes policy-based traffic steering into SR domains, enabling coordinated DDoS mitigation, QoS enforcement, and traffic engineering through BGP rather than per-device commands—essential for carrier and hyperscaler networks.

BGP Flow Specification (FlowSpec) distributes traffic filtering and steering rules across BGP networks. This draft specifies BGP FlowSpec procedures to steer matching traffic flows into Segment Routing Policies, defining two modes: transport steering into an SR Policy (Mode 1) in SR-MPLS and SRv6 networks using BGP Extended Communities, and in SRv6 networks, transport steering combined with egress service actions using BGP Prefix-SID Attribute. The draft is currently in IETF Last Call with an associated November 2026 milestone. Two additional working-group drafts in RFC Editor queue include draft-ietf-6man-sidlist-clarification (clarifies SRv6 SID list processing) and draft-ietf-spring-resource-aware-segments. For network operators, this closes a critical gap in SRv6 deployments: previously, steering required static SID lists or controller-imposed paths; now BGP flowspec can dynamically redirect traffic to any SR Policy endpoint, enabling carrier-grade traffic engineering.

Why it matters Enterprises with deployed generative AI systems in EU markets face a 12-day deadline for technical implementation of AI detection markers—policy alone will not satisfy compliance; requires systems engineering work now.

The EU AI Act's bifurcated implementation calendar—amended by the Digital Omnibus entering force July 27, 2026—creates a staggered enforcement timeline. For enterprises, December 2 is a hard technical deadline—compliance requires watermarking, metadata insertion, or detection flags embedded in system outputs and cannot be satisfied through legal documentation or policy statements alone.
