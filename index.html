BGP hijack redirects Chinese traffic to gambling sites

Tribune Online · Nov 11, 2026 · Industry news

Malicious actors exploited BGP to announce false routes through AS 402425, redirecting traffic from multiple Chinese provinces to unauthorized gambling sites using longest-prefix-match manipulation and DNS tampering without device infection.

Why it matters Demonstrates that route hijacking remains a practical threat despite RPKI deployment; operators must filter suspicious ASNs and validate BGP announcements at the origin level.

On November 11, 2026, China's CNCERT reported a sophisticated BGP hijacking attack where malicious actors used foreign autonomous system AS 402425 (registered in April 2026 by US-based Qorexa Labs) to announce false routes targeting multiple Chinese provinces. The attack leveraged the longest-prefix-match rule to override legitimate routes, redirecting traffic to unauthorized gambling destinations or malicious DNS servers without infecting end-user devices. The hijacked route flapped continuously throughout the approximately 22-hour incident, creating intermittent diversions. Route measurements showed roughly 72% of networks seeing the diverted traffic at peak moments, making it a broadly visible (though intermittent) hijack. CNCERT urged international registries to review and revoke malicious AS resources and recommended operators filter routes containing AS 402425. The incident underscores that BGP origin validation alone (RPKI) does not prevent path manipulation—ASPA (Autonomous System Provider Authorization) deployment is needed to validate AS relationships in the complete path.

The Hacker News · Nov 11, 2026 · Industry news

eMudhra revoked 8 additional certificate authorities after discovering a Python script exposed RSA private keys to process memory during HSM transfer. Six were for TLS, two for code signing, with 195 total certificates issued including 29 from eMudhra's own domains.

Why it matters Network teams relying on eMudhra certificates must urgently audit certificate chains before browser root programs remove distrusted roots; applications using WebPKI CAs for mTLS face forged-certificate authentication bypass risks.

eMudhra discovered its key-wrapping ceremony script left RSA private key material—private exponents and prime factors—resident in process memory on the ceremony computer. The company had placed four of the affected intermediate CAs into production in May 2026. This compounds an October discovery that eight eMudhra intermediates had cryptographically weak keys generated by a broken random-number mechanism. Microsoft is removing eMudhra root certificates from all products starting November 16, 2026; Google Chrome and Mozilla Firefox have set SCTNotAfter removal to the same date. Network teams must identify all services using eMudhra-issued certificates and plan replacement before browsers distrust them. Applications using WebPKI roots in mutual TLS scenarios now face direct authentication bypass: attackers holding either of the two compromised code-signing CAs can forge certificates to authenticate to mTLS endpoints that trust the web PKI.
