Kentik compares twelve network monitoring platforms for 2026

Kentik · Sep 29, 2026 · Analysis

Kentik published comprehensive 2026 network monitoring tool evaluation covering 12 platforms including Kentik, Datadog, SolarWinds NPM, LogicMonitor, ThousandEyes, Dynatrace. Kentik leads for AI-powered network intelligence; LogicMonitor for broad hybrid monitoring with AIOps; ThousandEyes for DEM and internet path visibility.

Why it matters Current practitioner evaluation framework shows network observability market segmentation: AI-first intelligence platforms vs. broad observability suites vs. digital experience monitoring, helping operators evaluate tool fit for NOC operations.

Kentik's 2026 network monitoring tools report categorizes the market into three groups based on team focus and use case. Kentik leads for AI-powered network intelligence across hybrid, multicloud, WAN, and internet-connected environments, with SaaS AI Advisor, cause analysis, custom network context, and AI-generated summaries. LogicMonitor positioned for broad hybrid infrastructure monitoring with AIOps-style alerting, anomaly detection, and event correlation—strong device coverage plus cloud monitoring, but can feel broad if network-specialized depth is priority. Cisco ThousandEyes leads DEM, SaaS assurance, and internet path visibility—outside-in visibility into internet, cloud, and SaaS dependencies; often complements rather than fully replaces core NPM/NMS tooling. The report also covers traditional NPM suites (SolarWinds, PRTG, ManageEngine), open-source options (Zabbix, OpenNMS), and cloud-native platforms (Datadog, Dynatrace, New Relic). The categorization directly addresses practitioner challenge: choosing between depth in network intelligence (Kentik), breadth in infrastructure observability (LogicMonitor, Datadog, Dynatrace), or outside-in internet/SaaS experience (ThousandEyes). Market segmentation shows consolidation pressure: vendors expand capabilities into adjacent domains while maintaining core strengths, requiring careful evaluation of integration, alert correlation, and AIOps maturity for NOC deployment.

RPKI adds negligible BGP update noise, study finds

APNIC Blog · Oct 2, 2026 · Primary source

Study provides strong evidence that RPKI is not a significant source of BGP noise, with RPKI-triggered updates showing negligible volume. Operators should not view routing stability as a major obstacle to RPKI deployment when ROAs are managed correctly.

Why it matters Addresses the common concern that RPKI deployment increases routing instability. Operators can confidently expand RPKI adoption without fearing destabilization from validation-triggered updates.

Study provides strong evidence that RPKI is not currently a significant source of BGP noise, with resulting update volumes being negligible. Growth in RPKI-related updates closely follows the growth of RPKI deployment itself. As ROV becomes more widely deployed, operational impact may continue to evolve, but continued monitoring remains important. After examining more than eleven years of measurements, the study concludes that RPKI's security benefits are achieved with remarkably small impact on routing stability. This is the most recent research on the critical question of whether RPKI deployment causes routing disruption, based on over a decade of data. Network operators implementing RPKI can reference this evidence when justifying investment in ROV configuration and ROA management to teams concerned about stability impacts.

Subsea cable cut disrupts Philippines to Singapore traffic

Rappler · Oct 3, 2026 · Industry news

Undersea cable cut between Philippines and Singapore on October 2 disrupted international traffic; PLDT and Converge ICT rerouted most traffic through alternative submarine cables while maintaining local service availability.

Why it matters Demonstrates real-world subsea resilience and traffic engineering responses in Southeast Asia, critical for multi-path strategy planning given regional concentration of recent cable incidents.

Internet users in the Philippines experienced slower and intermittent connections on October 2 after undersea cable segment linking Philippines and Singapore went offline, affecting services nationwide. PLDT successfully rerouted most affected traffic. Converge ICT rerouted internet and data traffic to Singapore through other submarine cables, with technical teams monitoring connections. DICT Secretary stated possible sabotage is among scenarios being investigated, but too early to draw conclusions; could be natural causes or equipment failure. This continues the pattern of high-impact subsea cable disruptions in Southeast Asia observed in August-September 2026 (Indigo West/Central, Australia-Singapore Cable). Operations teams should review Southeast Asia routing dependencies and validate fast failover to alternative subsea paths via Japan, US, or other regional routes given concentration of incidents in the region.

Check Point VPN gateways exploited through pre-auth RCE flaw

Bleeping Computer · Oct 2, 2026 · Industry news

Check Point confirmed active exploitation of CVE-2026-85102, a pre-authentication RCE in VPN certificate handling affecting Security Gateways and Spark Firewalls since September 12. Attacks originate from anonymized infrastructure using crafted VPN certificates; CISA added both CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103 to KEV catalog with federal patch deadline of September 25.

Why it matters Zero-day-like exploitation of perimeter VPN infrastructure demands immediate patching for gateway administrators managing remote access; certificate-based bypass undermines trust model for edge security.

Check Point confirmed active exploitation of CVE-2026-85102, a pre-authentication RCE in Security Gateway VPN certificate-handling since September 12, 2026, with attackers using VPNs and proxies to hide their location. Attackers used VPN certificates with subjects CN=vpn,OU=users,O=global; CN=vpn-user,OU=users,O=global; and CN=vpnuser,OU=users,O=global. The flaw involves improper validation of certificate data during VPN negotiation on Security Gateways and Spark Firewalls, while CVE-2026-85103 is a heap overflow in ASN.1 certificate decoding affecting Security Gateways, Security Management Servers, and Spark Firewalls. Both vulnerabilities (CVSS 9.8) allow unauthenticated remote code execution. CISA added both flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, requiring federal agencies to patch by September 25, 2026. Recommended remediation includes installing Check Point LivePatch Take 26 or applying Jumbo Hotfixes (R81.20 Take 166, R82 Take 126, R82.10 Take 44, or R81.10 Take 190).

Cisco patches 97 CVEs including exploited FMC flaw

Greenbone · Sep 26, 2026 · Industry news

Cisco released 97 CVE fixes in September 2026, largest coordinated disclosure to date, with 18 fixes to Secure Firewall (ASA/FTD/FMC) including multiple critical unauthenticated RCE flaws running as root. CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10) targeting FMC was added to CISA KEV with evidence of active exploitation campaigns in August affecting 178 devices.

Why it matters Mass firewall RCE patches require prioritized lab testing and staged rollout; CVE-2026-20079 active exploitation on FMC justifies emergency patching ahead of routine change windows for sensitive environments.

September 2026 marked Cisco's largest coordinated vulnerability disclosure period to date, with 97 new CVE IDs spanning major enterprise security and networking products; 32 are umbrella CVEs meaning the actual number of underlying software flaws is unknown. Cisco fixed 18 Cisco Secure Firewall vulnerabilities on September 16, 2026, with several critical flaws allowing remote code execution as root spanning Firewall Management Center, ASA, and Threat Defense software. CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0) is an authentication bypass in Cisco Secure FMC web interface allowing unauthenticated remote attackers to execute script files and obtain root access. Cisco identified active exploitation in August 2026 with three clusters of post-compromise activity (UAT-12197, UAT-11823, UAT-11988) deploying web shells and malware, with more than 3,000 IP addresses targeted and 178 devices infected with PivotC2. Among the 29 CVE IDs affecting Secure Firewall products (ASA, FTD, FMC), eight are rated critical severity indicating unauthenticated, remotely exploitable issues.

Agentic AI agents start replacing NetOps ticket queues

The Network DNA · Oct 2, 2026 · Analysis

Agentic NetOps uses autonomous AI agents to monitor, diagnose, plan, and remediate network issues with minimal human intervention, reasoning and acting rather than simply detecting and alerting. The article contrasts agentic systems with traditional AIOps, covering practical adoption patterns and failure modes.

Why it matters Clarifies architectural distinction between AIOps (detection/alerting) and AgenticOps (reasoning/execution); guides practitioners on safe adoption guardrails and domain boundaries.

Agentic NetOps uses autonomous AI agents powered by LLMs and real-time telemetry to correlate events, form hypotheses, run diagnostics, propose or execute fixes, and verify outcomes—unlike traditional AIOps which mostly detects and alerts. The piece addresses the operational reality: first-wave AIOps delivered anomaly detection and noise reduction but stopped at dashboards. Agentic NetOps closes that gap but introduces new risks. The article walks through where agents deliver value today (triage, validation, RF optimization, approval-gated remediation) versus where they fail (blind config pushes, cross-domain cascading failures). Critical for NetDevOps and network SREs: the architecture requires live network context, domain-specific reasoning, and guardrails that scale with blast radius. Network changes propagate across campus, data center, WAN, and cloud—a single agent error cascades faster than on compute infrastructure.

New RFC extends STAMP to asymmetric network paths

RFC Editor / IETF · Sep 30, 2026 · Primary source

RFC 10052 standardizes performance measurement with asymmetrical traffic using the Simple Two-Way Active Measurement Protocol (STAMP), published as a Proposed Standard in September 2026. Extends STAMP capabilities to handle asymmetric network paths where forward and return paths have different characteristics.

Why it matters Essential for network operators measuring real performance across asymmetric paths—critical for cloud, ISP, and WAN telemetry operations using STAMP probes.

RFC 10052 adds performance measurement support for asymmetrical traffic patterns to STAMP, addressing a practical gap in operational telemetry. Network operators have long relied on STAMP (defined in RFC 8762) for active two-way measurement, but most deployments encounter asymmetric paths where return-path latency, loss, or jitter differs significantly from forward path. This RFC formally extends STAMP to characterize these asymmetries, enabling more accurate SLA verification and root-cause analysis for application performance issues. The specification includes measurement modes, formatting, and interpretation guidelines for asymmetric scenarios. Implementation in vendor products and open-source tools like gNMIc-based collectors will follow, making this immediately actionable for ops teams using telemetry-driven monitoring at scale.

OpenTelemetry JavaScript SDK 3.0 slips to October, requires Node 22

OpenTelemetry Project GitHub · Oct 2, 2026 · Primary source

OpenTelemetry JavaScript SDK 3.0 release date postponed to October 15, 2026, with feature-freeze on 2.x as of September 1. Raises minimum Node.js requirement to v22+ (EOL for v20 was April 30, 2026), adds dual CJS/ESM exports via tsdown, and aligns Prometheus exporter with OpenTelemetry specification.

Why it matters SRE and AIOps teams must plan Node.js runtime upgrades and SDK migration timelines; v3.0 drops support for older runtimes and fixes metric semantics, requiring dependency updates across observability infrastructure.

SDK 3.0 cleans up the public API surface, drops unmaintained packages, raises minimum runtime requirements, and aligns closely with the OpenTelemetry specification. The feature-freeze on 2.x (September 1, 2026) gates new instrumentation features behind a major version bump, forcing teams to choose between legacy Node.js support and modern capabilities. With Node.js v20 reaching end-of-life April 30, 2026, requiring v22+ eliminates support burden for deprecated runtimes and standardizes on actively maintained versions. Dual CJS/ESM exports via tsdown migration resolve long-standing module compatibility issues that have hindered adoption in mixed ESM/CJS codebases. The Prometheus exporter alignment means metric semantics and cardinality handling now match the OpenTelemetry specification, eliminating surprising behavior in high-cardinality scenarios common in large-scale telemetry collection. Teams should plan v3.0 adoption now to avoid surprises post-October 15 release.
