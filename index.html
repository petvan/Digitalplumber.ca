Kentik adds agentic AI that investigates network problems autonomously

Network World · Nov 18, 2026 · Industry news

Kentik launched AI Advisor on November 18 as an agentic AI system that formulates network investigation plans, executes queries across telemetry sources, and presents findings with supporting evidence to automate root cause analysis.

Why it matters Agentic autonomous network investigation reduces NOC triage time by replacing manual query iteration with AI-driven RCA: system formulates investigation plans, queries data, and validates findings—turning raw telemetry into structured JSON recommendations.

Kentik is taking the next step in its AI journey with AI Advisor, launched November 18. The autonomous investigation capability required Kentik to extend its data platform beyond flow records and device metrics. The Kentik Data Engine processes approximately one trillion telemetry points daily from NetFlow, sFlow, device APIs, cloud provider APIs, and synthetic monitoring. Kentik uses commercial large language models (LLMs) rather than training its own from scratch, with development effort focused on training the system to understand network operations workflows rather than tuning model weights. Post-generation validation checks determine whether recommendations make logical sense given the network state. Because the system outputs structured data in JSON format rather than natural language, Kentik can programmatically verify recommendations. This represents a shift from correlation-only AIOps to agentic workflow automation: the system learns network operations APIs and engineer practices, then autonomously plans multi-step investigations without engineer context-switching.

Latent gateway defect and capacity shortfall caused 24-hour Microsoft 365 outage

365Fanatic · Nov 20, 2026 · Primary source

Microsoft released final post-incident report for 12-13 November outage: latent defect in July gateway fix combined with Teams failover logic and 14% capacity shortage caused 24-hour global Exchange and 365 services loss.

Why it matters Classic infrastructure cascade demonstrates how dormant code defects, failover behavior, and thin capacity margins align to cause global outage. Ops teams should audit gateway failover assumptions and capacity reserves.

On 12 November 2026 at 16:20 UTC, Exchange Online and other Microsoft 365 services became unreachable globally; impact ended 13 November at 16:10 UTC, incident resolved 14 November at 04:15 UTC. Root cause: a routing issue in US East gateway region triggered Teams connection timeouts; Teams auto-failover concentrated workloads onto the shared Exchange gateway, but that gateway had a latent connection-handling defect from July 2026 deployments. With 14% capacity offline and peak load hitting remaining gates, the system could not shed load. Microsoft reverted the defective change at 07:20 UTC on 13 November. The incident illustrates how interdependent failover logic can amplify modest failures: a single defect is survivable, failover capacity is survivable, but both together with thin margins causes cascade.

Attackers exploit hard-coded credentials in Cisco Expressway

CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog · Nov 20, 2026 · Primary source

CISA added CVE-2026-76466, a critical CVSS 9.8 vulnerability in Cisco Expressway that allows unauthenticated remote code execution as root, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 20, 2026; attackers compromised four devices and attempted lateral movement in a Russian organization.

Why it matters Expressway sits between internal collaboration systems and the internet, holding trust relationships and credentials that reach well past the device; exploitation enables full UC infrastructure compromise and lateral movement into internal networks.

CVE-2026-76466 allows unauthenticated attackers to remotely execute arbitrary OS commands as root on affected Cisco Expressway Series devices configured in clusters, exploiting hard-coded credentials in persistent cookies. Cisco first disclosed the vulnerability October 21 with CVSS 9.8; fixed software was available before CISA confirmed exploitation, with a federal patch deadline of November 23. Cisco Expressway Series Release X15.5.20 and later are fixed. Researchers at F6 reported attackers compromised two internet-facing devices and reached two internal cluster members through one of them; security teams should check internal cluster members even if not directly reachable from the internet. Any internet-exposed Expressway cluster without X15.5.20 or later faces immediate compromise; isolation and rapid patching are critical.

Hong Kong regulator wants kill switches for banking AI agents

Bloomberg Professional · Nov 24, 2026 · Industry news

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority launched the ART (Accountability, Resilience, and Trust) Framework establishing supervisory expectations for AI adoption and agentic AI workflows in banking. The HKMA will release a discussion paper proposing intent-anchored accountability, an industry registry for material AI agents, and intervention mechanisms such as automated kill switches.

Why it matters Banks must designate clear business owners for each AI use case and maintain centralized inventories to prevent shadow AI, establishing board-level accountability for AI strategies.

The HKMA's ART Framework represents the first jurisdiction-level governance framework specifically addressing autonomous AI agents in financial services. The framework combines three pillars: Accountability (requiring board-level oversight and designated business owners for each AI use case), Resilience (covering data governance, vendor management, cybersecurity), and Trust (ensuring consumer fairness and systemic stability). The framework mandates enterprises maintain centralized AI inventories to prevent uncontrolled shadow AI deployments—a critical compliance gap identified across global institutions. Notably, the HKMA is not creating a parallel regulatory structure but building on existing supervisory frameworks, reducing compliance overhead. The upcoming discussion paper will propose specific requirements for agentic systems, including intent-anchored accountability clarifying which human is responsible for each autonomous decision, a central registry for material AI agents, and automated intervention mechanisms like kill switches. This follows Singapore's January 2026 Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI, making Hong Kong the second major jurisdiction to establish dedicated agentic AI governance rules.

TechCrunch · Nov 23, 2026 · Industry news

Amazon is acquiring Decart's AI optimization technology and research team for $7.5 billion. Decart's Optimization Stack (DOS) software enables AI models to run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon, and Google hardware through compiler-level optimization.

Why it matters The acquisition strengthens AWS Trainium competitiveness by coupling custom silicon with software optimization, allowing customers to reduce inference costs and latency on non-Nvidia chips.

Amazon's $7.5 billion acquisition of Decart represents a strategic bet on software-driven AI chip efficiency competing against Nvidia's near-monopoly. The deal includes founders Dean and Orian Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev, and the engineering team, with performance-based milestone payments over five years. The acquisition excludes Decart's proprietary AI models. This follows Amazon's failed attempts: Anthropic walked away from a $6 billion offer in September 2026, and Nvidia (which had invested $300M at $4B valuation in May) also declined to acquire. For practitioners, AWS Trainium customers gain access to optimization capabilities previously unavailable on custom silicon, potentially reducing inference costs and latency significantly. The strategic significance extends beyond AWS—by coupling custom silicon with software optimization, Amazon can offer competitive performance without matching Nvidia's raw compute density, reshaping competitive dynamics in the AI infrastructure market.
