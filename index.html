The analysis demonstrates internet-scale observability and cross-layer correlation of network and application failures.

Why it matters Shows how modern NOC teams use internet-scale telemetry to detect and validate outages independent of vendor status pages, enabling faster incident response and root cause validation.

ThousandEyes' published analysis documented the Microsoft 365 outage starting at 17:30 UTC on November 12, 2026, when its global monitoring detected TCP connection resets, timeouts, and internal packet loss across Microsoft's infrastructure. The telemetry showed Exchange Online traffic experiencing significant degradation, with continued impact through the recovery period on November 13. ThousandEyes' Internet Insights service, which aggregates anonymized data across its customer base to detect broader internet disruptions, provided independent validation of outage scope and timeline—confirming the incident affected services globally well before Microsoft's formal status updates. The analysis illustrates the operational value of deterministic network observability: NOC teams using ThousandEyes could correlate application-layer failures (Outlook timeouts, 503 errors) with network-layer symptoms (packet loss, connection resets), providing evidence-based incident validation without relying on vendor statements. This real-world outage demonstrates how observability tools enable faster escalation decisions and verify when problems are resolved.

Why it matters Demonstrates how network observability platforms provide quantified, time-stamped evidence of service impact severity—critical for SLO validation, incident severity classification, and post-incident root cause investigations in multi-tenant environments.

Kentik's analysis, led by Doug Madory (Director of Internet Analysis), tracked the Microsoft Exchange Online outage using BGP-level and application-layer network telemetry. The analysis quantified the incident's scope: Exchange Online traffic dropped from expected levels to 85% below baseline starting at approximately 17:35 UTC on November 12, providing objective proof of outage onset independent of user reports or internal Microsoft metrics. Kentik's continuous monitoring captured the recovery phase on November 13, showing traffic returning to normal at 11:20 UTC, with subsequent queue recovery pushing traffic 19% above expected as delayed messages caught up. For NOC teams managing SLAs, this independent quantification enables non-repudiation: the traffic data serves as forensic evidence of when services actually failed and recovered, separate from any internal or vendor claims. The ability to measure impact in network-native metrics (traffic volume, BGP routing changes, latency) gives operations teams a technical foundation for escalation decisions, customer communications, and post-incident reviews—especially critical when vendors like Microsoft face scrutiny over timeline accuracy during major incidents.

BGP hijack via AS 402425 redirects Chinese traffic to gambling sites

tribuna.com · Nov 11, 2026 · Industry news

Malicious actors exploited BGP longest-prefix-match rules to announce false routes through AS 402425 (registered to US-based Qorexa Labs in April 2026), hijacking traffic across Chinese provinces and substituting DNS servers without infecting end-user devices.

Why it matters Demonstrates continued BGP hijack risk despite RPKI; highlights operator filtering gaps and AS registration abuse as a vector for regional traffic interception.

Malicious actors exploited BGP rules to announce false routes through foreign AS 402425, registered in April 2026 by Qorexa Labs. The attack leveraged longest-prefix match to override legitimate routes, substituting official website addresses with gambling-site destinations or malicious DNS servers without infecting end-user devices. While measurements pinpointed tampering within AS 402425, investigators noted that analysis does not establish intent on the part of the company. CNCERT urged international registries and IP-address trading platforms to review and revoke malicious AS resources, while recommending that operators filter out routes containing AS 402425. This incident reinforces the need for strict egress filtering at regional boundaries and rapid coordinated response to malicious ASNs.

eMudhra revokes eight more CAs after private key blunder

BleepingComputer · Nov 11, 2026 · Industry news

eMudhra revoked eight additional intermediate CA certificates after discovering improper handling of private keys during certificate generation, where a script reduced eight ECDSA private keys to a single byte. Chrome and Firefox will stop trusting emSign G1-chained certificates as of November 16, 2026.

Why it matters Certificate holders must immediately reissue TLS certificates from unaffected CAs to avoid trust failures; infrastructure teams need immediate CA trust store and certificate inventory audits.

Following October's discovery of 8 eMudhra intermediate CAs with trivially weak keys, the November investigation found a second round of CA compromise: 8 RSA intermediate CAs exposed to process memory during HSM transfers due to a flawed in-house Python script. While these 8 RSA keys were not cryptographically weakened like the October batch, they were nonetheless revoked November 7 on advice from browser root programs. The dual compromise now totals 16 affected intermediate CA certificates. Chrome, Firefox, and Safari are removing emSign G1 root certificates from their trust stores on a phased basis: Chrome and Firefox November 16, Apple November 30. Organizations holding certificates chaining to emSign G1, emSign ECC G3, or G2C intermediates face either immediate replacement or certificate rejection by browsers and TLS clients. This represents a systemic PKI failure affecting tens of thousands of organizations globally, particularly in higher education and government sectors. Infrastructure teams must prioritize certificate reissuance from DigiCert, Sectigo, or other established CAs, and verify certificate chains in production systems before November 16 cutoff dates to prevent service outages.

Critical FortiOS routing flaw and FortiManager RCE patched

Red Tiger Security · Nov 10, 2026 · Industry news

Fortinet released November 2026 advisories fixing a critical FortiOS routing flaw (CVE-2026-126253, CVSS 9.3) enabling unauthenticated attackers to trigger firewall routing announcements, and critical FortiManager flaws including unauthenticated code execution (CVE-2026-126261, CVSS 9.0) via the FGFM management channel.

Why it matters Network operators running FortiOS or centralized FortiManager must assess attack surface; routing flaws require OSPF network proximity but JSON-RPC API flaws expose systems if API is enabled.

Fortinet's November 10 release addresses 17 CVEs with three critical for network infrastructure. CVE-2026-126253 (OSPF auth bypass, CVSS 9.3) allows unauthenticated attackers on the same network segment running OSPF to craft packets causing unauthorized routing announcements—a control-plane threat for internal network hijacking. CVE-2026-126170 (JSON-RPC JSON injection, CVSS 9.8) affects FortiManager and FortiAnalyzer when JSON API is enabled, allowing unauthenticated code execution on the management console. CVE-2026-126261 (FGFM service auth bypass, CVSS 9.0) enables attackers reaching the FortiGate-to-FortiManager port to execute code. As of November 12, none show confirmed exploitation in the wild and none appear in CISA KEV, but critical severity makes these priority. Network teams should inventory FortiManager deployments and JSON API enablement status before scheduling patches. Routing flaws affect only MX Series hardware and specific HA configurations, reducing blast radius compared to centralized management vulnerabilities.

CISA adds exploited OpenSSH and FreeSwitch flaws to KEV

The Hacker News · Nov 9, 2026 · Industry news

CISA added CVE-2026-123158 (OpenBSD OpenSSH SSHole authentication bypass, CVSS 8.1) and CVE-2026-116779 (SignalWire FreeSwitch code injection, CVSS 9.8) to the KEV catalog on November 9. Exploitation of SSHole was first observed in honeypots November 7, 2026, with active attacks ongoing since November 6.

Why it matters Infrastructure teams must immediately patch OpenBSD OpenSSH on Linux systems; exploitation is currently active with honeypot hits confirmed by GreyNoise. Any internet-exposed SSH services need emergency patching.

CVE-2026-123158 (SSHole, CVSS 8.1) is an unexpected status code or return value vulnerability in OpenBSD OpenSSH on Linux enabling authentication bypass. GreyNoise documented first exploitation on November 7 at 12:46 UTC with activity accelerating since November 6. The flaw affects OpenBSD OpenSSH packaged for Linux distributions; internet-facing SSH services need immediate patching with November 2026 fixes available. CVE-2026-116779 (SignalWire FreeSwitch, CVSS 9.8) is a code injection vulnerability enabling configuration disclosure, credential theft, arbitrary call control, and remote command execution—critical for VoIP environments. CVE-2026-102895 (GPUStack, CVSS 9.8) and CVE-2026-123218 (IBM Aspera Faspex, CVSS 9.8) represent broader authentication and authorization bypasses affecting ML infrastructure and enterprise file transfer. Network teams should prioritize OpenSSH patching as immediate action 1, then FreeSwitch if VoIP is deployed, followed by other KEV entries on risk-based schedule aligned with system criticality and internet exposure.

Over half of organizations run agentic AI in production NetOps

Harvard Business Review / Cisco · Nov 11, 2026 · Vendor release

Over half of organizations already use agentic AI to act on NetOps processes and tasks in production, per a 2026 Omdia/Cisco study of 1,000 IT leaders. Enterprise monitoring systems generate over 4,000 alerts daily, roughly half network-related, driving the shift to autonomous triage and action.

Why it matters As network teams hand authority to AI agents, the challenge is deciding what to trust them with and ensuring verification costs less than manual work—a practical governance model for production NetOps.

The Cisco/Omdia research reveals rapid adoption of agentic NetOps in production environments. Half of surveyed organizations are already deploying AI agents to handle network operations tasks autonomously. The core business driver is alert fatigue: with 4,000+ daily alerts and growing complexity from AI workloads adding new traffic patterns and dependencies, manual triage is unsustainable. However, the research emphasizes a critical operational constraint: the cost of verifying an agent's work must be lower than performing the work manually. This frames the real decision boundary for NetOps teams—not whether to automate, but which tasks pass the verification cost test. The study shows that organizations are moving beyond pilot phases and deploying agents in production, suggesting frameworks for safe autonomy are emerging. The tension between speed and verification remains central to adoption rates.

Why it matters Operations teams need to prepare BSS/OSS platforms for autonomous AI decision-making in customer lifecycle management, requiring new auditability and consent mechanisms for production deployments.

Southeast Asia AI funding more than doubles to $4.2 billion

TNGlobal (Tracxn) · Nov 12, 2026 · Research

Southeast Asia AI companies raised $4.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026—up 121 percent year-over-year—with the largest round being Kling AI's $2.8 billion Series D. The ten largest rounds accounted for 98 percent of all SEA AI funding.

Why it matters SEA AI funding concentration is extreme: mega-rounds dominate (top 10 = 98% of capital), seed funding declined, and 100% of SEA AI funding came from Singapore-based companies—meaning infrastructure and adoption patterns are highly consolidated and geographically concentrated.

Tracxn's comprehensive 2026 data reveals structural shifts in Southeast Asia's AI ecosystem. The headline 121% year-over-year growth masks a capital concentration problem: Kling AI's $2.8B Series D (video/content), Sharpa's $670M Series D (backed by Alibaba, Meituan, Tencent), and Zelostech's $300M Series C dominated, while the 98% concentration in top-10 rounds signals that mid-stage and early-stage companies are starved of funding. Seed funding rose 28% in value but fell in count (10 rounds vs. 18 prior year), suggesting fewer early-stage opportunities. On the exit side: limited activity—one acquisition (Meta bought Manus in April 2026 for undisclosed sum), one IPO, no new unicorns. Investor count dropped from 69 to 56 year-over-year; 39 were first-time investors. Geographic concentration is near-total: 100% (vs. 99% prior year) came from Singapore-based companies, indicating negligible activity outside the city-state. For practitioners evaluating SEA as an AI adoption region, this concentration suggests infrastructure, talent, and vendor ecosystems are centered in Singapore with limited regional diversification.
