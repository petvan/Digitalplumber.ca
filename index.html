HashiCorp adds governed Terraform workflows for AI agents

HashiCorp / IBM · Oct 26, 2026 · Primary source

HashiCorp released new Terraform capabilities enabling humans and AI agents to safely operate infrastructure at scale with governed workflows and connected intelligence through HCP Terraform and Infragraph. Focus on agentic infrastructure operations with task-level automation replaced by trusted governance models.

Why it matters Shifts Terraform from declarative IaC to agent-ready orchestration platform—operators need to understand how governance, state visibility, and MCP integration change their CI/CD change management workflows.

HashiCorp's October 26 announcement signals a strategic pivot: Terraform is evolving beyond static infrastructure provisioning into a foundation for AI-assisted and AI-driven operations. The new capabilities center on three themes: Scale (connected infrastructure intelligence via Infragraph), Safety (governed workflows with built-in approval gates and audit trails), and Agent Integration (MCP-native support for AI agents to propose and execute changes). For network operators, this is significant because it means Terraform state becomes an observable, queryable source of truth that AI systems can reason about—not just execute changes against. The governance layer is critical: as agents become active infrastructure operators, organizations need approval gates, rollback procedures, and audit evidence. HCP Terraform's remote state and policy-as-code now provide these guardrails at the platform level rather than requiring custom middleware. The Infragraph capability generates domain-specific intelligence about infrastructure topology and dependencies, enabling safer what-if analysis. For practitioners deploying network infrastructure with Terraform, this suggests moving toward GitOps-style workflows where proposed changes are validated by policy before agents apply them, with full audit trails for compliance.

Meta open sources liquid-cooled 102.4T AI network racks

Engineering at Meta · Oct 26, 2026 · Primary source

Meta shared networking innovations supporting its latest AI models and gigawatt-scale Prometheus cluster at OCP Summit 2026. IcePack and IceCube are Meta's liquid-cooled network racks featuring 102.4T switch blades with 128×800G ports using Broadcom Tomahawk 6C and NVIDIA Spectrum-6 variants, running FBOSS.

Why it matters Meta's liquid-cooled Tomahawk 6C and Spectrum-6 fabric platforms at 102.4T capacity demonstrate production-ready 800G deployment patterns for hyperscale AI clusters at gigawatt scale.

Meta's OCP presentation detailed production fabric layers for the Prometheus cluster: IcePack and IceCube are the first shipping liquid-cooled fabric racks integrating 102.4 Tbps switch density—each rack houses 128 ports of 800G, achieved via dual ASIC support (Broadcom Tomahawk 6C or NVIDIA Spectrum-6). Running Meta's FBOSS switching stack, these represent the first documented production deployment of Tomahawk 6 silicon at hyperscale. In the scale-up tier, Tahan is Ethernet-based on Broadcom Tomahawk5, engineered for MTIA 300 accelerators at 72 per rack. Meta's ESUN switching software progressed from 1.0 to 1.1, reflecting operational maturity. The strategic significance: both Tomahawk 6 and Spectrum-6 achieve equivalent 102.4T throughput, validating open silicon paths and eliminating vendor lock-in while operating at production density. For practitioners, this confirms both platforms are operationally stable for gigawatt-scale buildouts; liquid cooling is now standard practice for 800G deployment at this density, with real-world FBOSS telemetry available for tuning RoCE congestion control and lossless Ethernet configuration.

Power failures drove most Internet outages last quarter

Cloudflare Blog · Oct 27, 2026 · Primary source

Cloudflare Radar examined Q3 2026 global Internet disruptions driven by power failures, natural disasters, and government shutdowns, finding that power dependency of routers, mobile base stations, and data centers was a leading pattern in disruption data.

Why it matters Understanding which failure modes—power, natural disasters, shutdowns—dominate regional outages helps operators prioritize resilience investments and redundancy placement.

The Internet's distributed design depends critically on electricity. When power fails, routers, mobile base stations, data centers, interconnection facilities, and the networks connecting them can fail simultaneously. This post examines Cloudflare Radar's outage detection data across Q3 2026 to identify patterns in what takes the Internet offline. For network operators and AIOps practitioners, this analysis provides concrete data on failure mode frequency: which regional outages stem from power infrastructure collapse, which from natural disasters (flooding, storms), and which result from deliberate government-mandated shutdowns. The findings help practitioners decide whether to invest in backup power, geographic redundancy across regions with different risk profiles, or peering diversity. Cloudflare's perspective is valuable because Radar observes traffic anomalies from millions of domains, not just a single provider's services, giving a broader Internet-scale view than typical vendor postmortems. The report also quantifies recovery time by failure type, which informs incident response planning and SLO design for infrastructure teams managing multi-region deployments.

CISA confirms active exploitation of two critical PHP flaws

CISA, The Hacker News, BleepingComputer · Oct 27, 2026 · Primary source

CISA confirmed active exploitation of two critical PHP vulnerabilities rated CVSS 9.2 facilitating unauthenticated remote code execution: CVE-2026-109618 (out-of-memory corruption) and CVE-2026-109619 (stale internal request-state). Successful RCE instances were confirmed on honeypot systems October 24, 2026.

Why it matters All versions of PAN-OS and Panorama are affected; any network appliance running PHP web management interfaces (firewalls, load balancers, SD-WAN platforms, orchestrators) faces immediate unauthenticated RCE risk. Federal agencies have until October 29 to patch.

CVE-2026-109618 is an out-of-memory corruption vulnerability allowing unauthenticated remote code execution; CVE-2026-109619 is a stale internal request-state vulnerability with identical impact. Both score 9.2 CVSS. PHP versions 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35 released October 21, 2026 address these flaws. Adel Karimi at Perplexity confirmed successful RCE on October 24, noting the flaw delivers usable memory disclosure and functional payload execution on single-worker mod_php setups. Palo Alto Networks confirmed all PAN-OS and Panorama versions are affected with no fixed versions available until October 30. Fortinet and Cisco published advisories indicating product investigations are ongoing. PHP 8.1 and older receive no vendor security updates; customers must seek OS vendor backports or migrate to supported versions. Network operators must immediately identify PHP-based management services, restrict access to trusted networks, and prioritize upgrades pending vendor patches.

Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day exploited for superuser access

Arista Security Advisory 0199, SecurityWeek, Aviatrix Threat Research · Oct 23, 2026 · Primary source

Arista disclosed an actively exploited CVSS 9.9 SQL injection zero-day (CVE-2026-114625) in VeloCloud Orchestrator, the central management plane for SD-WAN networks. Any authenticated user—even read-only accounts—can escalate to superuser operator privileges. This is the third VCO zero-day exploited since July 2026.

Why it matters SD-WAN orchestrators are internet-exposed control planes governing thousands of branch and cloud sites. Three zero-days in four months signals sustained targeting; on-premises VCO compromise enables SD-WAN fabric takeover and lateral movement across branch and cloud infrastructure.

CVE-2026-114625 is an SQL injection vulnerability in VeloCloud Orchestrator On-Prem deployments allowing any authenticated user with basic privileges to escalate to operator-level privileges via low-complexity SQL injection, effectively masquerading as a local superuser. Arista published Security Advisory 0199 on October 22, 2026, noting detection of active exploitation. Fixes shipped the same day across three release trains: VCO 5.2.3.18, 6.1.3.10, and 6.4.2.12. Two attacker-controlled IPs (74.82.197.86 and 38.54.50.151) were observed exploiting the vulnerability before patches became available. Cloud-hosted VCO instances were patched by Arista automatically. The escalation pattern—unauthenticated flaws followed by authenticated privilege escalation—suggests attackers are systematically chaining VCO weaknesses to achieve persistent, high-privilege control over enterprise SD-WAN fabrics. Network operators must immediately upgrade on-premises deployments, audit orchestrator logs for attacker IPs, hunt for webshells and backdoor daemons, and validate all VeloCloud Edge device configurations for unauthorized changes.

OpenTelemetry Transformation Language reaches stable version 1

OpenTelemetry Blog · Oct 27, 2026 · Primary source

OpenTelemetry Transformation Language (OTTL) is now v1, becoming stable for traces, metrics, and logs starting with opentelemetry-collector-contrib v0.164.0. The v1 release meets the Collector's 'stable' stability criteria for testing, benchmarking, documentation, and telemetry, with a compatibility promise for all of 1.x.

Why it matters Operators standardizing on OTTL for data pipeline transformations now have a stable, backward-compatible contract for traces, metrics, and logs without concern for breaking changes in 1.x minor releases.

As of October 27, 2026, OTTL v1 is available in opentelemetry-collector-contrib v0.164.0 and is stable for traces, metrics, and logs. OTTL syntax and standard functions remain backward compatible, and the Go API for components built on top of OTTL also maintains compatibility. The v1 promotion involved a comprehensive review of every token, type, and feature in the grammar, with all documented language reference elements now frozen for 1.x, covering paths, literals, operators, function calls, and where clauses. New syntax can be added in minor releases, but existing syntax will not be removed or changed in meaning. Some limitations remain: lambda-powered functions (Filter, MapEach, MapKeys, Any, All, Find, Reduce, and When) are still experimental and require the ottl.functions.enableLambda feature gate. For practitioners building operational telemetry pipelines, this signals a mature component suitable for production deployments where consistency across software versions is critical.

New RFCs extend PCEP for segment routing multipath

RFC Editor · Oct 26, 2026 · Primary source

RFC 10066 defines PCEP extensions to signal multipath information, associating multiple forwarding paths with a single PCEP LSP for load balancing and redundancy across diverse paths. RFC 10067 extends PCEP support for Segment Routing to group two unidirectional SR LSPs into an associated bidirectional SR LSP.

Why it matters Network operators can now signal multiple equal-cost or weighted paths per LSP and coordinate bidirectional SR paths via PCEP, enabling more granular traffic steering, load balancing, and redundancy at the segment routing layer without requiring separate signaling for each direction.

RFC 10066's primary application is Segment Routing Policy, where a Candidate Path can contain multiple segment lists; current PCEP extensions only allowed signaling of a single segment list per Candidate Path, but these extensions now enable multipath capabilities such as weighted or equal-cost load balancing across paths. RFC 10067 extends the procedure from RFC 10066 to allow grouping two unidirectional SR LSPs into an associated bidirectional SR LSP for both co-routed and non-co-routed paths, utilizing the procedure from RFC 10066 to carry multiple EROs and associated reverse path EROs for an SR LSP. Both RFCs were published October 26, 2026, and represent a maturation of SR control-plane capabilities for operators deploying carrier-grade traffic engineering at scale. These enable finer-grained path optimization and redundancy without requiring parallel signaling channels.
