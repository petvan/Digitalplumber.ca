LLM use doubles but network automation stays stuck at a third

Ray Mota's Substack · Nov 20, 2026 · Analysis

Practitioner analysis of the 2026 State of Network Automation Survey presented at AutoCon 6 by Chris Grundemann. While LLM adoption doubled year-over-year, overall network automation rates remained flat at approximately one-third, with dedicated automation specialists declining from 25% to 17% of respondents—indicating broader adoption but less specialist focus.

Why it matters Authoritative industry benchmark showing automation adoption stalled while LLM interest surged—critical for practitioners planning tool investments and staffing. Explains why agents cannot solve adoption speed without addressing organizational and skills-gap barriers.

The 2026 State of Network Automation Survey, the fourth annual from the Network Automation Forum, was presented live at AutoCon 6 on November 17-20, 2026. Survey respondents numbered 344 automating teams across 58 countries and 34 industries. The headline finding repeated from 2025: approximately one-third of networks remain automated. However, LLM adoption doubled year-over-year within that cohort, while dedicated automation engineers and developers fell from almost 25% of the sample to 17%—meaning the respondent pool became less specialist. The gap between these two trends reveals the story: broader participation in automation through AI tools, but persistent lack of specialized staffing. The survey also captured a 52.9-point gap in troubleshooting automation: 66.3% say troubleshooting should be automated, but only 13.4% have implemented it. When asked what holds teams back, only 10% cited technical challenges; 44% pointed to people—skills gaps (27%), organizational resistance (20%), and cultural friction (14%). This data challenges assumptions that agents alone will accelerate automation adoption without addressing organizational structure and training.

RAG system turns plain-language intent into industrial device CLI configs

arXiv / Springer · Nov 25, 2026 · Research

Peer-reviewed research on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods that translate network engineers' natural-language intent into executable CLI configuration commands. System achieved 0.94 Answer Relevancy and 0.77 ID-Based Context Recall with 25-second average generation time for industrial network device management.

Why it matters Practical IBN implementation using LLM + RAG that works for real devices—demonstrates feasibility of natural-language-to-CLI translation at production latency, directly relevant to practitioners evaluating intent-based automation platforms.

Published in *Program Comput Soft* 52, 542–551 (2026) and indexed in Springer, this peer-reviewed paper presents the first published RAG-based method for translating natural-language network intent into executable device CLI commands. Intent-Based Networking (IBN) addresses the challenge of abstracting network engineers from device-level configuration details by introducing a management paradigm centered on operational intent. The researchers evaluated their system on industrial network configurations and measured Answer Relevancy (0.94) and ID-Based Context Recall (0.77), with an average response generation time of 25 seconds. This latency is acceptable for change staging but not for real-time operations, indicating the approach works for pre-deployment validation and planning rather than reactive changes. The paper received June 8, 2026, underwent revision June 8, and was accepted July 27, 2026, with final publication November 25, 2026. It represents academic validation of IBN concepts—intent translation, CLI generation, and configuration validation—that vendors like Itential, Forward Networks, and Cisco are building into production platforms. The specificity of benchmark results makes this substantive for practitioners evaluating whether LLM-driven IBN can reach production reliability.

BGP hijacks divert 1.9 EH/s of bitcoin mining traffic

Braiins Blog · Nov 25, 2026 · Primary source

An attacker rerouted internet traffic to redirect bitcoin miners on unencrypted Stratum V1 connections to an imposter pool. On November 20, Braiins recorded four episodes affecting 1,050–1,600 sites and 0.6–1.9 EH/s per episode for 6–49 minutes.

Why it matters Networks enforcing RPKI route origin validation rejected the forged announcements, but most networks do not validate routes. This demonstrates uneven RPKI deployment leaves operators vulnerable to path-level attacks that origin validation alone cannot stop.

Braiins documented coordinated BGP route hijacking attacks targeting bitcoin mining pools between November 7–26, 2026. The campaign operated in two waves: an initial phase from November 7–12 affecting mostly Latin America (Brazil, Argentina) with 19 episodes of 36–174 PH/s each; followed by a larger European phase on November 20 with four coordinated episodes simultaneously affecting 1,050–1,600 mining sites per burst, diverting 0.6–1.9 exahashes per second. The attack required no compromise of mining hardware, pool systems, or DNS infrastructure. Instead, attackers announced a more-specific /24 BGP prefix within a legitimate /22 IP block, exploiting longest-prefix-match and widespread unencrypted Stratum V1 mining protocol. When miners reconnected after a TCP reset, the attacker's server accepted any login and relayed work to another pool while crediting shares to the attacker's account.

Networks enforcing RPKI route origin validation rejected the forged announcements outright and experienced no loss of hashrate. However, the attack persisted because the forged route listed the legitimate address owner as its origin, defeating hijack detection systems relying solely on origin validation without path security. This demonstrates a critical gap: RPKI ROV adoption remains uneven across transit networks, and the absence of path-level security (BGPsec) leaves the vast majority of networks vulnerable. Braiins released firmware patches (Braiins OS 26.11), a free Stratum V2 proxy for incompatible hardware, and emphasized that only TLS with certificate hostname verification or Stratum V2 stops this class of attack.

CISA flags Mitel MiCollab flaws exploited before patches existed

CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog · Nov 25, 2026 · Primary source

CISA added five Mitel MiCollab flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 25, led by an unauthenticated command injection in the Audio, Web and Video Conferencing component rated CVSS 10.0. Accenture assesses threat actors exploited these vulnerabilities before the patch existed, with web shell activity documented before November 15.

Why it matters The 10.0 command injection allows unauthenticated arbitrary command execution with no workarounds; federal agencies must patch by November 28, and evidence shows active exploitation predates disclosure.

CVE-2026-133323 scores 10.0 and allows arbitrary command execution without authentication in MiCollab's Audio, Web and Video Conferencing component. In one observed attack, an attacker ran the first command 12 minutes after initial access, reached root within 30 minutes, and accessed domain controller credentials within 32 minutes. Accenture APEX Labs found evidence of exploitation on November 15, before Mitel's official November 16 advisory, suggesting the flaws were weaponized before patches existed. Mitel disclosed the vulnerabilities on November 16 and updated its advisory with CVE identifiers on November 24. The batch includes four other exploited flaws: CVE-2026-133275 (file upload, CVSS 5.3), CVE-2026-133303 (XML injection, 9.0), CVE-2026-133311 (weak authentication, 5.9), and CVE-2026-133324 (failure to fail securely). All five can be exploited without prior authentication, and Mitel states they can be chained together. CISA lists November 28 as the remediation deadline for four vulnerabilities and December 9 for CVE-2026-133311. Affected versions are MiCollab 10.3 and earlier.

Bundled PHP flaws expose network appliances from eight vendors

InfraTrust threat intelligence; CSA Singapore advisory; Zerohour.day · Nov 26, 2026 · Analysis

InfraTrust's November 2026 report revealed systemic supply chain vulnerability affecting Palo Alto Networks, Check Point, Fortinet, Juniper, Cisco, Arista, SonicWall, and others via PHP2Shell (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619). Threat actors chained these PHP flaws with Linux kernel CVE-2026-46300 to gain root privileges and deployed data-wiping malware on VMware ESXi.

Why it matters As of November 20, patches for PHP2Shell were either partially available or pending for Fortinet, Juniper, Lenovo, HPE, Netgear, HP and Siemens, leaving broad infrastructure exposed. Upstream open-source vulnerabilities cascade across dozens of downstream products simultaneously, compressing the patch window.

PHP2Shell consists of two PHP vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) affecting bundled PHP in network appliances. Active exploitation chains these with CVE-2026-46300, a Linux kernel privilege-escalation flaw, to gain root privileges and persist across reboots. Check Point confirmed attackers deployed data-wiping malware on ESXi hypervisors post-compromise. Palo Alto Networks reported activity against exposed management interfaces; both Palo Alto and Check Point have confirmed exploitation. The flaws also impact network products from Cisco, Citrix, F5, Siemens and Fortinet. As of November 20, patches ranged from partial availability to pending release across vendors. On November 19, an additional 18 PHP security advisories were released, further straining patch-management capacity. The incident illustrates structural supply-chain risk: when a critical open-source component is bundled into infrastructure software, upstream vulnerabilities create simultaneous downstream exposure across dozens of products, and vendors' patch-integration windows become a primary attack surface as AI accelerates both discovery and weaponization.

Malicious PyPI builds of Microsoft agent framework steal Azure credentials

JFrog Security Research · Nov 25, 2026 · Research

Unauthorized versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 of Microsoft's agent-framework package were uploaded to PyPI on November 17, 2026, containing Python .pth startup hooks that harvest Azure identities, CI/CD tokens, and AI developer secrets on every Python launch.

Why it matters Operations teams using agent frameworks in CI/CD must immediately audit lockfiles and container images; this attack undermines trust in open-source agent tooling and demonstrates supply-chain risk in agent harnesses.

Unauthorized versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 of Microsoft's agent-framework package went live on PyPI on November 17, 2026. Version 1.26.1 landed at 20:35 UTC and was quarantined 31 minutes later; the attacker uploaded 1.26.2 two minutes after, and both were removed by 21:16 UTC. Compromised versions use Python .pth startup hooks to harvest Azure identities, CI/CD tokens, and AI developer secrets on every Python launch. The attacker left the agent-framework-core dependency pin untouched so the framework kept working without errors, appending a silent _telemetry._start() call and adding a 28-byte .pth file to site-packages. The attack repeats on every Python process launch unless _AF_TEL is set, ensuring credential exfiltration across the entire CI/CD pipeline. Microsoft issued a security advisory recommending teams rotate credentials available to affected environments and rebuild from clean images. JFrog Curation and JFrog Xray detect and block compromised versions before they enter build pipelines.

Curvature-based congestion pricing cuts routing computation cost

arXiv · Nov 24, 2026 · Research

Proposes topology-aware regularization of congestion pricing by augmenting Pigouvian tolls with structural penalties derived from Forman-Ricci curvature, achieving reductions comparable to edge betweenness at substantially lower computational cost.

Why it matters Addresses how traditional Pigouvian congestion pricing loses effectiveness under demand shifts at structurally critical bottleneck edges—applicable for traffic engineering teams optimizing network paths under uncertainty.

Classical Pigouvian tolls achieve system optimum only under known demand and lose effectiveness when demand shifts, particularly at structurally critical bottleneck edges. This work proposes augmenting precomputed Pigouvian tolls with an offline structural penalty derived from Forman-Ricci curvature (FRC), which captures bottleneck structures in the network topology. FRC achieves comparable reductions to edge betweenness centrality at substantially lower computational cost, making it practical for large networks. The topology-aware regularization of the Beckmann potential governing Wardrop equilibrium provides operators with a robust congestion management strategy that works under varying demand patterns without requiring real-time demand prediction.
