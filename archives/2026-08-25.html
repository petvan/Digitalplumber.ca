MicrosoftWindows PatchingEntra IDCISAPatch Management

Under CISA risk-based timelines, internet-facing Windows systems required patching by August 14, with internal endpoints due August 25, 2026. A critical detail: kernel driver patches require system restarts—a patch installation report showing 100% deployed but 0% rebooted is not actually patched. Microsoft's August update covered approximately 400 vulnerabilities, continuing a trend of sustained high-volume releases (July saw ~570 CVEs). For Entra ID, Microsoft is phasing out SMS and voice MFA: September 1 begins automated passkey enrollment nudges; February 1, 2027 is the hard enforcement date with no opt-out. Organizations with regulatory constraints requiring SMS/voice must document that need now. The operational impact cuts across AIOps and SRE teams—patch automation frameworks must now track reboot status separately from installation status, and identity automation must factor passkey deployment into baseline access controls.

NVIDIA Adds Scale-In as Fifth Pillar of AI Factory Networking

NVIDIA is adding Scale-In as the fifth pillar of its AI networking architecture, using BlueField-4 DPUs, DOCA software and Spectrum-X Ethernet to accelerate north-south access, security, storage and infrastructure operations for agentic AI factories. Scale-In targets the north-south side of the AI factory—the path connecting accelerated compute with users, applications, enterprise data, external storage, cloud services and security systems. BlueField-4 provides the processing foundation for offloading network and storage operations from host CPU.

BlueField-4 DPUScale-InDOCASpectrum-X EthernetInfrastructure

Scale-In represents an architectural pivot from treating DPUs as optional infrastructure acceleration to positioning them as mandatory for gigascale deployment. BlueField-4's role is to offload network and storage operations from host CPU, allowing GPUs and CPUs to focus on compute and inference while DPUs handle encryption, traffic management, and network segmentation. This formalizes a trend operators have observed empirically: as cluster size scales toward 100K+ GPUs, CPU cores consumed by networking/security overhead become a binding constraint, making dedicated DPU processing architecturally mandatory rather than optional. BlueField-4 enters early deployment in 2026 as a core component of NVIDIA Vera Rubin platform, available as PCIe card or integrated into NVL144 rack systems, delivering 8 EFLOPS per liquid-cooled rack.

SDxCentral Aug 24, 2026 Standards

ITU backs Japanese framework for low-latency all-photonic networks

ITU-T standardized a low-latency photonic network framework from Japanese operators including KDDI, NTT, and NEC, designed to support low-latency and energy-efficient connections between all-photonic networks run by different operators.

The International Telecommunication Union's standardization sector (ITU-T) formally approved a standardized framework for all-photonic networks (APNs) developed collaboratively by seven Japanese firms including KDDI, NTT, and NEC. The framework addresses low-latency and energy-efficient connectivity between APNs operated by different carriers—critical as operators transition from traditional opaque networks to transparent photonic architectures. For infrastructure teams, this standardization establishes common reference models and control plane interfaces that reduce vendor lock-in and enable multi-operator network slicing at the photonic layer. For SRE and NetOps practitioners, this signals the industry push toward all-optical infrastructures handling latency-sensitive demands of AI agent orchestration and real-time cloud-native workloads without electronic switching bottlenecks. The framework emphasizes energy efficiency—essential as data center networks struggle with power density constraints from AI cluster growth.
