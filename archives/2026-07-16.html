The Hacker News Jul 15, 2026 Industry Trend

SASE Has An AI Blind Spot: Inspecting Packets Is No Longer Enough

Traditional SASE architectures relying on cloud proxy inspection fail to secure modern workflows where data interactions now happen at the browser and application layer via AI agents and SaaS apps. Network-centric enforcement cannot govern what occurs inside application tabs or LLM interactions, requiring a shift to endpoint-based policy enforcement and dynamic edge routing.

SASEZero TrustAI SecurityBrowser SecurityEndpoint Security

SASE has proven effective for routing and basic traffic inspection, but the architectural model breaks down when enterprise workflows migrate to browsers, SaaS applications, and AI agents operating at machine speed. Modern encryption protocols (TLS 1.3, HTTP/3, certificate pinning) explicitly block the man-in-the-middle decryption that traditional SASE depends on, leaving security teams facing a binary choice: block AI entirely and push users to shadow IT, or allow unrestricted access with total data opacity. The core problem is that sensitive data now flows through presentation layers—copy/paste into LLMs, prompt interactions, automated agent queries—which network-level proxies cannot see. The guidance is clear: policy enforcement must shift to the device (browser and endpoint), where contextual data protection can inspect copy, paste, and prompt content before it leaves the device. Dynamic edge steering for traffic that does require network routing eliminates redundant hops and performance degradation. This represents a structural paradigm shift from network-centric to application-layer-aware security, requiring enterprises to rethink how zero trust actually works when data interactions happen outside the network perimeter entirely.

Finance Institutions Race Ahead on AI Security Automation—But Breach Rates Tell a Darker Story

A Gigamon survey of 139 finance security leaders reveals that 66% of financial institutions deploy AI security automation with no human in the loop—well above the 53% industry average—yet 77% experienced breaches involving AI and detection speeds are slowing, not improving. The mismatch suggests finance automated response before automating visibility.

AIOpsAI SecurityFinancial ServicesBreach DetectionVisibility

Financial services firms are leading enterprise adoption of autonomous AI security tools, with 91% running AI-powered data protection and two-thirds allowing those tools to act independently. This appears to be a success story for AIOps maturity until the breach data surfaces: 77% of surveyed institutions experienced AI-involved breaches, 54% saw AI-written phishing and smishing escalate, and 47% faced attacks targeting their own AI deployments. Critically, detection is getting slower despite higher automation. The root cause is visibility: 95% of finance security leaders admit their telemetry lacks visibility into data in motion that detection systems depend on. Compounding this, 88% identify harvest-now, decrypt-later collection as a present threat, 36% rank encrypted traffic as their single greatest breach vulnerability, and 93% tie post-quantum readiness to visibility gaps inside encrypted channels. The verdict is stark—these institutions automated the response layer before securing the observation layer, meaning their autonomous tools inherit whatever blind spots their telemetry has. Attackers exploit both simultaneously: the models making decisions and the traffic nobody watches. The implication for practitioners: autonomous AI security amplifies whatever the system cannot see; before buying the next tool, prove the AI you already trust can actually see what it is acting on.
