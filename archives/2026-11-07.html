Cloudflare halves DNS build delay and cuts memory 85%

Cloudflare Blog · Nov 2, 2026 · Primary source

Zone setting changes used to rebuild entire DNS zones with tens of millions of records; Cloudflare built a targeted planner that picks the smallest safe build, cutting full builds by 40%, peak memory by 85%, and halving build delay.

Why it matters Cloudflare skipped full rebuilds on 92.95% of DNS builds over 24 hours, showing where large-platform gains come from: classifying internal changes precisely enough to skip expensive work without serving stale data.

Cloudflare's November 2 post describes a targeted build planner and new scheduler that combine work for the same zone; zone setting changes no longer rebuild zones with tens of millions of records, cutting peak memory by 85% and halving the time from DNS change to Quicksilver write. The Zone Builder now receives about 700 DNS record changes per second (up from 250 in 2022), plus about 100 other events per second such as zone setting changes. Over 24 hours, 86.81% of builds touched resources only and 7.04% required full zone builds, with remaining full builds averaging 0.35 seconds. The 85% peak-memory cut and halved median build delay are internal pipeline measures ending at Quicksilver write, representing capacity and tail-latency gains inside Cloudflare rather than end-to-end propagation time for individual domains.

Cloudflare DNS rebuilds run twice as fast

Tom's Hardware · Nov 4, 2026 · Industry news

Cloudflare's engineers halved DNS zone rebuild times and cut memory usage by 85% across its network through scheduler and planner optimizations, following earlier 100 TB memory savings in DNS cache and task-server routing.

Why it matters Large DNS operators should apply targeted build classification techniques to avoid full zone rebuilds; Cloudflare's approach shows how to achieve 3x throughput increase (250 to 700 changes/sec) without proportional resource growth.

The optimization combined two separate planning and implementation schedulers into one improved scheduler; Cloudflare changed how the scheduler acts when Apache Kafka messages trigger slow builds, fulfilling them shortly after issuance in consistent cadence, improving concurrency capacity and tightening queue processing intervals, dropping median build time from 7.7 seconds to 3.3 seconds. Peak per-server memory dropped from 50-79 GB to close to 7 GB. This represents the continuation of Cloudflare's systematic approach to DNS infrastructure efficiency, relevant to operators managing large authoritative DNS platforms with millions of records per zone and high change rates. The architectural insight is classifying zone changes by impact scope rather than rebuilding entire zones, allowing skip of expensive work while maintaining correctness.

Chained Ivanti zero-days give unauthenticated root on Connect Secure

Ivanti Security Advisory / CISA KEV / SecurityWeek · Nov 2, 2026 · Primary source

CISA added CVE-2026-111357 and CVE-2026-118527 to its KEV catalog after Ivanti patched two Connect Secure zero-days chained with CVE-2026-104935 in active attacks. Three chained vulnerabilities deliver unauthenticated root-level code execution on any unpatched appliance, including those running the most current software before today.

Why it matters Attacks chaining the three flaws had hit a limited number of customers by the time they were disclosed, but the chain enables complete remote compromise of remote access gateways; requires immediate upgrade to patched versions.

CVE-2026-111357 (CVSS 8.8) is a missing-authentication flaw enabling an authenticated attacker with local access to elevate privileges. CVE-2026-118527 (CVSS 4.3) involves predictable identifier generation, allowing an authenticated attacker to remotely upload files containing content of their choosing to known disk locations. Ivanti patched the initial authentication bypass CVE-2026-104935 on October 13, then shipped fixes for CVE-2026-111357 and CVE-2026-118527 on October 29. The chain achieves remote, unauthenticated command execution followed by privilege escalation to root; Ivanti and CISA confirm exploitation in the wild. Federal agencies were given until November 2 to address CVE-2026-111357 and until November 13 for CVE-2026-118527. Fixed versions are 25.1.1.3 or later and 22.8R2.6 for the two newly patched flaws.

Zyxel GS1900 switch zero-day exploited against 996 devices

Binarly / GreyNoise / Infosecurity Magazine · Nov 3, 2026 · Industry news

Binarly detailed a buffer overflow flaw affecting Zyxel GS1900 switches in research published November 3, linking it to an exploit GreyNoise described in September targeting 996 switches in 48 countries. The vulnerability now carries identifier CVE-2026-107441, and a previous patch did not block this exploit.

Why it matters Zyxel rates CVE-2026-107441 at 8.8; Binarly rates it 9.8 with network-level access possible; unpatched network edge devices in production remain compromised across multiple geographies.

Binarly discovered that Zyxel's June firmware patch for GS1900 switches did not block the exploit used against 996 switches; the vulnerability now carries CVE-2026-107441. The flaw is a buffer overflow rated 8.8 by Zyxel. GreyNoise reported in September that an attacker had exploited this flaw against 996 Zyxel GS1900 switches in 48 countries, taking switch configurations, hashed root credentials and networking data. Zyxel rates CVE-2026-107441 at 8.8 and says an attacker must be on the local network to exploit it, while Binarly rates it 9.8 and says exploitation requires only network access to the switch's management service. CISA added CVE-2026-7273 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day. The discrepancy in severity ratings and access requirements reflects ongoing debate about exploitation complexity, but the global scope of prior compromise signals urgent patching need for GS1900 models across all customer bases.

The model can find and build working zero-days in real-world critical systems and found 81 high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities and 948 Linux exploits in testing.

Why it matters First frontier model openly rated for vulnerability discovery and exploit development; establishes precedent for capability disclosure and access control under AI preparedness frameworks.

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on November 5, 2026, rated Critical under its Preparedness Framework—one tier above GPT-5.6 Cyber's High rating. By OpenAI's definition, the Critical tier means a model can find and build working zero-days in real-world critical systems and run attacks end to end. In internal testing, the model found 81 high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities and 948 Linux exploits. Access is restricted to vetted security customers to prevent misuse. This marks the first OpenAI model to receive the Critical rating, and the public disclosure of the rating—rather than keeping it confidential—signals a transparency shift in how the industry discusses model capability tiers. For practitioners, this demonstrates that frontier models now routinely include capabilities that can materially affect infosec posture. GPT-6 Cyber's controlled access and Preparedness Framework rating provide a template for how labs are attempting to gate high-risk capabilities, though enforcement and definition of "vetted" remain implementation details.
