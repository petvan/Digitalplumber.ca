Network Automation Market Projected to Reach $12.38B by 2030 at 9.4% CAGR

MarketsandMarkets research projects the global network automation market will grow from $7.88B in 2025 to $12.38B by 2030, driven by multi-cloud complexity, hybrid IT, IoT expansion, and edge computing. The analysis highlights intelligent network automation as the fastest-growing segment, with SDN, NFV, and intent-based networking accelerating adoption across enterprises.

Network AutomationMarket GrowthAI/ML IntegrationMulti-cloudIntent-Based Networking

The network automation market is experiencing accelerated growth driven by converging forces reshaping enterprise infrastructure. Multi-cloud strategies, hybrid IT environments, IoT proliferation, and edge computing are creating unprecedented network complexity that manual management cannot scale to meet. Organizations are increasingly turning to automation to achieve faster provisioning, reduce human error, and optimize resource utilization—all critical for managing distributed, heterogeneous environments. Within the market, intelligent network automation (leveraging AI, ML, and analytics for real-time optimization) is expected to grow at the highest CAGR. This segment represents a fundamental shift from script-driven or rule-based automation toward systems that can predict issues, recommend configurations, and dynamically adjust resources. The adoption of software-defined networking (SDN), network function virtualization (NFV), and intent-based networking (IBN) platforms is amplifying this trend, as these technologies inherently require programmable control planes and policy enforcement at scale. Rising cybersecurity threats and compliance requirements are also driving automated monitoring, patching, and governance—functions that are increasingly mandatory rather than optional. The proliferation of 5G networks and edge infrastructure is creating vast, distributed, low-latency environments that cannot be managed through traditional CLI/GUI methods, accelerating the business case for automation across service providers and enterprises alike.

TechCrunch Aug 28, 2026 Industry Trend

Federal judge voids Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as supply chain risk, finds unlawful First Amendment retaliation

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled on August 28, 2026 that the Trump administration's designation of Anthropic as a national security supply chain risk was illegal, constituting unlawful First Amendment retaliation and arbitrary action. The designation followed Anthropic's refusal to remove safety guardrails that would allow Pentagon use for fully autonomous weapons and mass surveillance.

AnthropicPentagonAI SafetyFirst AmendmentLitigation

In a 59-page order, Judge Lin found the Pentagon's designation 'illegal and baseless,' writing that the invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish government critics. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's move, made in February 2026 after Anthropic refused contractual terms permitting unrestricted military use of Claude, effectively blocked the company from federal defense contracts and cost it billions in lost business and reputational harm. The designation was the first time any U.S. company was publicly labeled a supply chain risk under the government procurement statute designed to protect military systems from foreign infiltration. Judge Lin found Anthropic's public criticism of the Pentagon's AI use policies was a substantial factor driving the government's actions, violating due process rights under the Fifth Amendment. Anthropic has a second pending lawsuit in Washington D.C. over a separate Pentagon supply chain risk designation affecting civilian government contracts. The ruling immediately constrains Pentagon authority to blacklist private AI companies based on policy disagreements.
