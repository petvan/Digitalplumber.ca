Lambda runs co-packaged optics across 10,000 Blackwell GPUs

CASRAI · Nov 16, 2026 · Industry news

Lambda and NVIDIA announced the first large-scale commercial deployment of co-packaged optics (CPO) networking, connecting over 10,000 Blackwell Ultra GPUs at Lambda's Quebec data center. The CPO fabric delivered 3.8% lower network latency at rack scale and 10.1% lower latency at pod scale compared to conventional pluggable optics.

Why it matters CPO switches operating at 800 Gb/s achieve median optics temperature of 36°C versus 51°C for pluggables, reducing thermal density and enabling denser AI fabrics without data center infrastructure redesign.

Lambda and NVIDIA announced a commercial CPO deployment at SC26 (November 16, 2026) connecting over 10,000 Blackwell Ultra GPUs in Quebec, Canada—the world's first verified commercial CPO deployment in an AI cloud environment. Co-packaged optics integrates optical engines directly onto the switch substrate beside the ASIC. Lambda's testing showed 3.8% lower rack-scale latency and 10.1% lower pod-scale latency versus pluggable optics with identical throughput. Each switch has 144 ports at 800 Gb/s with 18 removable external light sources, achieving median optics temperature of 36°C (versus 51°C for pluggables) and a PUE of 1.2 with water-free cooling. For operators: this is the first production verification that CPO deployments close the latency gap with pluggable optics while delivering the power and thermal benefits promised at smaller scales. CPO remains only ~0.5% of AI data center optical modules in 2026, rising toward 35% by 2030, so this deployment signals production readiness ahead of mass adoption and shifts the dial on feasibility for large-scale AI cluster fabric architectures.

Broadcom shows Tomahawk 6 and Thor Ultra at SC26

CASRAI · Nov 15, 2026 · Industry news

Broadcom demonstrated Tomahawk 6 and Thor Ultra 800G Ethernet silicon at SC26, highlighting compatibility with Ultra Ethernet Consortium (UEC) specifications for AI workloads. Thor Ultra eRoCE NICs achieve job completion times matching 800G InfiniBand quantum fabrics by placing packets directly into GPU memory.

Why it matters Open Ethernet silicon with UEC compliance and direct GPU memory placement eliminates packet ordering dependencies, allowing switches to spray packets across all links and closing the performance gap with InfiniBand at Ethernet scale and cost.

Broadcom showcased Tomahawk 6 switches (51.2 Tbps) and Thor Ultra 800G network interface cards at SC26, integrating emerging specifications from the Ultra Ethernet Consortium (UEC) and Open Compute Project's Scale-Up Ethernet Transport (SUE-T). Thor Ultra provides 800 Gbps per port with eRoCE support, direct GPU memory placement, and packet trimming capabilities, eliminating head-of-line blocking inherent in traditional RoCE. Technical analysis confirms that Tomahawk 6 combined with Thor Ultra achieves job completion times matching 800G InfiniBand Quantum fabrics. The silicon supports Credit-Based Flow Control and Link Layer Retry for intra-rack accelerator memory fabrics. For practitioners planning large-scale AI clusters: this demonstrates that open Ethernet fabrics have resolved the ordering and congestion issues that previously forced InfiniBand adoption, while maintaining multi-vendor sourcing flexibility and operational simplicity over proprietary solutions.

FRRouting bgpd flaw crashes all BGP sessions

NetDEF/FRRouting CNA · Nov 13, 2026 · Primary source

A flaw in FRRouting's bgpd component allows improper validation of the data field length in a BGP NOTIFICATION Cease/Hard Reset message, existing in FRRouting releases 8.3 through 10.5.1. This causes a heap buffer over-read and an assertion failure, leading to a crash of the bgpd daemon and denial of service by terminating all BGP sessions.

Why it matters Affects operators running FRRouting as their BGP daemon; allows remote DoS via crafted NOTIFICATION messages from established BGP peers with Graceful Restart capability.

In FRRouting (FRR) 8.3 through 10.5.1, bgp_notify_decapsulate_hard_reset() in bgpd/bgp_packet.c does not validate the length of the data field of a received BGP NOTIFICATION Cease/Hard Reset message (RFC 8538). The flaw occurs when processing a BGP NOTIFICATION Cease/Hard Reset message with a one-byte data field, causing a heap buffer over-read and an assertion failure, leading to a crash of the bgpd daemon and denial of service by terminating all BGP sessions. The vulnerability requires a remote attacker with an established BGP session and Graceful Restart capability negotiated with the Notification bit set. Upgrading to version 10.0.5, 10.1.5, 10.2.6, 10.3.4, 10.4.3, 10.5.2 or 10.6.0 eliminates this vulnerability. If upgrading is not feasible, disable the Graceful Restart BGP capability on both the local router and its peers using "no bgp graceful-restart notification" or "bgp graceful-restart-disable". Operators running FRRouting should prioritize patching to prevent bgpd crashes from established BGP peers.

OpenSSL discloses five low-severity QUIC and AES-GCM-SIV flaws

OpenSSL · Nov 10, 2026 · Primary source

OpenSSL published security advisory on November 10, 2026, documenting five low-severity vulnerabilities in QUIC (CVE-2026-95691, CVE-2026-88822), AES-GCM-SIV (CVE-2026-95692, CVE-2026-88823), and CMS (CVE-2026-107496). Fixes ready in GitHub; official releases scheduled December 1, 2026.

Why it matters QUIC use-after-free and AES-GCM-SIV issues affect all supported OpenSSL versions; organizations using HTTP/3, QUIC proxies, or edge infrastructure must plan library upgrades by December 1 release.

CVE-2026-95691 is a heap-use-after-free in the QUIC client triggered by a malicious QUIC server, potentially causing denial of service or information disclosure. CVE-2026-95692 is an out-of-bounds read and incorrect IV length handling in AES-GCM-SIV (affecting OpenSSL 4.1, 4.0, 3.5 only). CVE-2026-107496 is an infinite loop in CMS_encrypt() with an EC group order of 1, affecting 4.1, 4.0, 3.5, 3.0, 1.1.1, 1.0.2. CVE-2026-88822 is uninitialized stack bytes during QUIC peer detection (4.1, 4.0, 3.5). CVE-2026-88823 is AES-GCM-SIV AAD forgery after zero-length incremental update (4.1, 4.0, 3.5). All fixes are in the public GitHub repository; infrastructure teams running HTTP/3 endpoints, API gateways, or QUIC-accelerated services should prepare for patching by the December 1 release date.

Agentic pentest framework ARTEX found on 1,465 hosts

ARTEX automates vulnerability discovery and exploitation using multi-agent frameworks with no human approval in the loop.

Why it matters Network defenders must shift from signature-based detection to behavioral telemetry: ARTEX and similar frameworks turn multi-step attacks into machine-speed intrusions requiring detection of timing patterns, chained requests, and rapid bursts across systems.

Cloud Security Alliance published analysis of ARTEX agentic pentest frameworks found on exposed staging servers. Censys counted 1,465 hosts exposing ARTEX consoles as of early November 2026, with 10.6% also hosting other threat tooling (Vshell, Cobalt Strike, Adaptix C2). ARTEX organizes planner/worker agents that continuously diff patches, draft exploits, and execute them without human approval. The framework leaves identifiable request patterns in logs: specific tool chains, source-map reads, rapid password-spray bursts, and chained API calls. For network operations and incident response teams, this means traditional signature-based blocking and manual triage are insufficient. Detection requires behavioral correlation across multiple systems, understanding how agents sequence tools over time, and recognizing timing anomalies that differ from human operator patterns. Organizations must implement cross-system observability and behavioral baselines for agent-driven attack detection.
