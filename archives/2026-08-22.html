Everyone Bought AI Observability. Nobody Owns Agent Behavior.

The AI observability market has fractured into four segments (infra telemetry, dev tooling, runtime security, autonomous remediation) following a wave of 2025-2026 acquisitions, but none of the major vendors have solved the critical gap: determining whether an agent actually behaved correctly in production. This fragmentation leaves a governance vacuum.

AIOpsAgent BehaviorGovernanceObservabilityAI Safety

Security Boulevard's August 20, 2026 analysis identifies a structural problem emerging in the observability market as vendors rush to acquire and integrate AI capabilities. The piece notes that while companies invested heavily in observability platforms (Palo Alto acquiring Chronosphere for $3.35B, vendors shipping agent observability features), the category has splintered into infrastructure telemetry, developer tooling, runtime security, and autonomous remediation—each owned by different vendors or platforms. The core issue: visibility into *what an agent decided to do* isn't the same as visibility into *whether that decision was correct*. No major platform today owns agent behavior validation at scale. This matters operationally because agents in production make autonomous decisions on customer data, infrastructure, and financial systems without the same audit trails, approval workflows, or rollback mechanisms that governed human-driven operations. Teams deploying agents now face a choice: accept blind spots in agent behavior governance, or stitch together incompatible tools. The piece references 2026 vendor releases from Honeycomb, Chronosphere, ServiceNow, and others, noting their focus remains on observing agent *activity*, not validating agent *correctness*. This is a practitioner-level concern that will drive the next wave of tooling.

Model Context Protocol Reaches Ecosystem Maturity with 97M Monthly SDK Downloads Across All Major AI Labs

Model Context ProtocolMCPInteroperabilityOpen StandardAgent Ecosystem

Since Anthropic open-sourced MCP in November 2024, it has been adopted by OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and dozens of developer tool companies, becoming the de facto connectivity layer for agentic AI. Benchmarks from major providers—Anthropic Claude, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon—confirm MCP's ecosystem dominance with over 97 million monthly SDK downloads by early 2026. MCP is an open standard defining how AI applications connect to external tools and data sources; instead of building custom integrations for each service, developers write one MCP server usable by any MCP-compatible AI host. By March 2026, MCP transitioned from experimental local-tool wiring to powering large-scale agent workflows. MCP enables any compliant AI agent to plug into calendars, Notion workspaces, code repositories, or enterprise databases using a consistent JSON-RPC-based protocol over Streamable HTTP. The protocol has become the foundational standard for agentic interoperability, with active SDKs from all major LLM providers and resolved production friction points around statelessness and enterprise compliance.

Agentic AI Multi-Agent Systems Fundamentally Transform Autonomous Network Control and Operations at Scale

The convergence of agentic AI and multi-agent systems is fundamentally transforming autonomous network control in distributed, large-scale cellular networks, with heterogeneous AI agents collaborating for autonomous decision-making, dynamic adaptation, and complex goal achievement while maintaining resilience and fault tolerance.

Agentic AIMulti-Agent SystemsNetwork Operations6GDistributed Intelligence

Agentic AI networking (AgentNet) is a novel AI-native networking paradigm relying on large numbers of specialized AI agents collaborating and coordinating for autonomous decision-making, dynamic environmental adaptation, and complex goal achievement. AgentNet has potential to facilitate real-time network management alongside capabilities for self-configuration, self-optimization, and self-adaptation across diverse, complex networking environments. Agentic AI systems evolve toward decentralized structures where heterogeneous AI agents, each with distinct skillsets, specialized knowledge, and optimization objectives, collaborate to achieve complex shared goals. This approach offers significant advantages over traditional centralized systems, which suffer from single points of failure, scalability constraints, and high latency. The resulting systems are inherently more resilient and fault-tolerant, as failure of one AI agent does not compromise operation of the entire system. AI techniques have been increasingly applied to network management tasks including optimization, fault detection, and predictive analytics. This architectural shift addresses the operational challenges of modern cellular networks where traditional rule-based automation cannot handle cross-domain dependencies and service assurance at scale.
