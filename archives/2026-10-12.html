Ninety percent of resolvers ready for DNSSEC root key rollover

APNIC Blog · Oct 9, 2026 · Analysis

Geoff Huston measures pre-rollover adoption of KSK-2024 (key tag 38696) across DNSSEC-validating recursive resolvers. Analysis shows ~90% of reporting resolvers have incorporated the new key into trust-anchor sets before the October 11 migration from KSK-2017, tracking real-time readiness via DNSSEC sentinel queries.

Why it matters Resolver operators and recursive DNS infrastructure teams must verify active trust-anchor configuration before October 11 to avoid cascading SERVFAIL errors across all DNSSEC-signed domains.

This piece synthesizes measurement data from DNSSEC probing infrastructure tracking which validating recursive resolvers have learned and installed KSK-2024 into their trust-anchor configuration sets. Huston notes the DNS root key is 'special' because it sits atop the DNSSEC chain of trust with no parent key validating it—every other key in DNSSEC has a parent. The analysis provides visibility into the actual state of the Internet's readiness for this planned key rollover. Around 90% of reporting resolvers have added KSK-2024 to their TA sets, but the remaining 10% represent significant risk: unmodified systems, read-only key stores, embedded appliances, and offline systems that haven't yet received the automated RFC 5011 updates. The piece emphasizes that seeing the key in a DNS response is insufficient; operators must verify the running resolver daemon has actually loaded key tag 38696 into active memory. Failures may not emerge until cached DNSKEY records expire, often 48+ hours after rollover, complicating post-mortems.

Packeteer adds automatic BGP route leak detection and withdrawal

GitHub (GrandArcher/Packeteer) · Oct 10, 2026 · Primary source

Open-source BGP route-leak detection feature for Packeteer enables operators to watch their own announced routes across public collectors (RIPE RIS, RouteViews) and external vantage points, automatically withdrawing leaked routes while flagging RPKI invalidity. Leak detection integrates with optional BMP and Routinator feeds for operator's own AS validation.

Why it matters Network operators can now implement automated leak-watching without manual monitoring, reducing operator load for route-leak incident response and enabling per-route safety actions (withdraw single route, not bulk session flap).

This GitHub issue describes a new feature for Packeteer, an open-source BGP route optimizer and monitoring tool, designed to address route leaks at the origin AS. The feature is scoped to optional leak-watch block (off by default) that watches Adj-RIB-Out feeds from the operator's BMP implementation and publicly available collectors (RIPE RIS Live, NLNOG RING looking glass). On detection of any route carrying the operator's community or any prefix the operator injected appearing in AS paths outside the operator's AS, Packeteer emits an event/alert and withdraws that single route—never bulk moves that could cascade. The implementation integrates RPKI validation (via optional Routinator feed) to flag injected or synthesized routes that would be RPKI-invalid if leaked, preventing scenarios like the Cloudflare January 2026 incident where automation changes led to broad export-policy overrides. Acceptance criteria include FRR lab validation with misconfigured export filters, false-positive elimination over full runs, and config rejection if synthesize is enabled without leak_watch. This addresses a concrete operational gap: most leak detection today is passive (Qrator, Jared Mauch's detectors) or manual filtering; automating per-route withdrawal at the originating AS is a departure.

Researchers detect BGP leaks and hijacks across multiple scales

ACM Internet Measurement Conference (IMC 2026) · Oct 12, 2026 · Research

Peer-reviewed paper by Loye, Bautista, and Fontugne published in IMC 2026 proceedings on multi-scale anomaly detection methods for BGP routing incidents. Addresses detection of route leaks, hijacks, and anomalous path behavior across multiple measurement vantage points using signal analysis techniques.

Why it matters Provides academic-grade techniques for identifying routing anomalies across time and topology scales, enabling practitioners to build more robust automated detection systems for leaks and hijacks beyond single-point monitoring.

This paper presented at IMC 2026 addresses a key operational challenge: route anomalies—leaks, hijacks, misconfigurations—manifest differently at different time scales and geographic vantage points. A single collection point may miss ephemeral anomalies that converge within seconds; conversely, aggregating observations across multiple RouteViews/RIS collectors requires careful correlation to avoid false positives from normal BGP churn. The multi-scale anomaly detection (MAD) approach combines longitudinal BGP signal patterns—update frequency, AS-path variance, origin AS stability, prefix size changes—with spatial vantage-point voting to separate transient convergence artifacts from genuine route leaks or hijacks. This builds on prior work differentiating ephemeral leaks (harmless convergence transients) from real incidents. For practitioners, this offers a framework for tuning alert thresholds and reduction strategies in BMP/NetFlow pipelines to catch leaks before they propagate globally while avoiding alert fatigue from normal routing behavior.

ByteDance runs RDMA software layer for large-scale AI training

arXiv · Oct 8, 2026 · Research

ByteDance reports production deployment of ByteExpress, a software communication layer over hardware RDMA, addressing challenges in distributed ML systems: variable-size messages, concurrent connections, bursty incasts, and service failures that hardware offload alone cannot solve.

Why it matters Production RDMA deployment insights directly applicable to datacenter infrastructure operations and high-performance computing networking at scale.

This paper documents real-world challenges deploying RDMA in production parameter-server-based inference and training systems at ByteDance. While RDMA can reduce communication overhead in distributed ML, naive TCP replacement fails under production workload patterns: variable message sizes require careful buffering, concurrent connections stress limited connection contexts, bursty incast patterns cause queue saturation, and transient service failures require robust recovery. ByteExpress, a software abstraction layer over RDMA hardware, addresses these gaps through intelligent message batching, connection pooling, flow control, and timeout handling. The paper provides benchmark results on real production topologies and workload traces, making it immediately relevant to network operators scaling distributed systems and AIOps teams managing high-performance datacenter infrastructure. Key architectural lesson: hardware offload alone is insufficient; software must handle operational complexity that hardware cannot see.

Moving target defence counters low-rate DoS on EV charging SDN

arXiv · Oct 8, 2026 · Research

Studies cyber-defense strategies for Software-Defined Networking (SDN) applied to electric vehicle charging infrastructure, focusing on low-rate Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks arising from IT/OT convergence using game-theoretic moving target defense.

Why it matters Security analysis of SDN-controlled critical infrastructure (EV charging) with game-theoretic defense mechanisms directly applicable to network operations and security architecture decisions in emerging autonomous and smart-grid domains.

SDN enables flexible, programmable control of EV charging infrastructure (EVCI), but introduces security gaps as IT and OT systems converge. This paper models low-rate DoS attacks (which evade threshold-based detection) and proposes moving target defense (MTD) using game theory to randomize network topology and routing. The game-theoretic framework allows operators to compute optimal defense strategies given attacker capabilities and resource constraints. Key contribution: quantifies the tradeoff between defense overhead (network reconfiguration cost) and attack effectiveness, enabling operators to set defense policies based on risk tolerance. Directly relevant for network teams managing hybrid IT/OT infrastructure, critical infrastructure operators, and security architects deploying SDN in safety-critical environments. Provides measurable defensive strategies rather than generic security principles.

Anthropic proposes binding government rules for frontier AI models

Anthropic · Oct 10, 2026 · Primary source

Anthropic published its Advanced AI Framework, proposing how governments should establish binding safety testing, incident reporting, and oversight regimes for frontier AI models. The framework includes mandatory risk assessment, model transparency reporting, security controls, and external expert evaluation requirements.

Why it matters Major AI labs are shifting from voluntary commitments to advocating for binding statutory regulation, reshaping enterprise compliance obligations across multiple jurisdictions and setting precedent for how safety governance should be legally enforced.

Anthropic's policy release comprises three documents: a narrative essay by Dario Amodei spanning five policy domains (safety regulation, macroeconomics, science acceleration, civil liberties, geopolitics), the Advanced AI Framework (safety and testing regime), and the Economic Policy Framework (labor disruption response). The Advanced AI Framework specifies mandatory obligations including risk assessment across cyber offense, CBRN risks, harmful manipulation and loss-of-control scenarios; model reporting with transparency before and after major deployments; security risk management through testing and protection; incident response protocols; and external expert input via independent evaluation and red-teaming. Critically, obligations apply to frontier model developers, not downstream users—meaning California, New York and Illinois state laws remain in force as a floor, creating cumulative compliance layers rather than preemption. Amodei stated Anthropic would grant independent evaluators permanent model access to verify safety commitments. This represents an explicit industry pivot away from self-governance toward government-enforced statutory frameworks, with direct implications for how organizations assess vendor compliance across overlapping federal and state regulatory regimes.
