AI data centre buildout spreads across Asia-Pacific and Gulf

TecKnexus · Oct 7, 2026 · Industry news

AI data centre capacity expanded across Asia-Pacific and the Gulf with interconnect and subsea capacity keeping pace. d-Matrix integrates Raptor XPUs with NVIDIA using NVLink Fusion for scale-up and Spectrum-X Ethernet for scale-out, while Qualcomm and Amazon develop custom silicon for AWS AI with 1.6 Tbps optical connectivity.

Why it matters Networking vendors warned of supply bottlenecks affecting US AI data centre builds, signaling procurement urgency for infrastructure teams planning cluster scaling.

This monthly infrastructure insights report captures Q3 2026 market consolidation in AI systems and networking. Key findings show heterogeneous accelerator ecosystems (d-Matrix + NVIDIA, Qualcomm + AWS) driving diversity in fabric choices—NVLink Fusion for scale-up, Spectrum-X Ethernet for scale-out, and custom silicon with 1.6T optical connectivity. The optical transceiver supply chain is tightening for US data center builds, indicating lead time pressures on 800G and 1.6T deployments. Equinix expanded managed interconnect offerings (Fabric One) and launched an inference-as-a-service program with NVIDIA, reflecting operator focus on geography-distributed AI workloads. The report underscores that 2026 marks transition from monolithic hyperscaler AI builds toward multi-vendor, multi-site, fabric-first infrastructure planning where Ethernet dominates scale-out and emerging alternatives challenge proprietary interconnects.

Infoblox closes Kentik deal to merge DDI with flow data

The New Stack / PVO Newsletter · Oct 3, 2026 · Industry news

Infoblox completed acquisition of Kentik in August 2026, uniting DNS/DHCP/IPAM authority with real-time network flow behavioral intelligence and AI-guided investigation for root cause analysis.

Why it matters Converges network observability architecture: IPAM/DNS state now correlates directly with BGP routing context and flow data, enabling faster RCA for routing-related incidents and subsea cable failovers.

The Kentik acquisition marks a significant shift in network observability architecture: traditionally split between IPAM/DNS operations (Infoblox) and behavioral flow analytics (Kentik), the integration now connects DNS query patterns, DHCP assignments, and IPAM state data directly with real-time flow records, BGP routing context, and Kubernetes metadata.

This correlation is essential for answering operational questions like whether a latency spike is caused by a misrouted destination or if a DHCP exhaustion cascaded into BGP flapping; for practitioners running AI-driven investigation, this combined dataset enables AI Advisor and Cause Analysis features to correlate signals that previously lived in separate tools. Kentik's documented support for OpenConfig/gNMI streaming telemetry and high-fidelity flow analysis makes it particularly relevant for networks running modern intent-based systems and AIOps platforms needing sub-second visibility into state changes.

CISA flags actively exploited Fortinet FortiMail path traversal flaw

CISA · Oct 1, 2026 · Primary source

CISA added CVE-2026-104286, a Fortinet FortiMail path traversal vulnerability, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog based on evidence of active exploitation. This type of vulnerability poses significant risks to federal enterprise security.

Why it matters BOD 26-04 requires federal agencies to prioritize rapid remediation of KEV Catalog vulnerabilities on publicly exposed assets; all organizations should apply risk-based patching urgency to perimeter mail gateways.

CISA's October 1 addition of CVE-2026-104286 represents active exploitation of Fortinet FortiMail path traversal functionality. This vulnerability allows remote unauthenticated attackers to traverse directories and access unauthorized files on affected mail gateways—a common initial foothold in perimeter attacks. Path traversal flaws in security appliances are particularly dangerous because mail gateways sit on trust boundaries and handle traffic admins assume is pre-filtered. The CVE requires network ops teams to inventory all internet-facing FortiMail instances, verify current versions, and prioritize patching ahead of standard SLAs. This follows earlier September 2026 KEV additions for Cisco Firewall Management Center and Citrix NetScaler perimeter gear, confirming sustained focus on network edge exploitation as attackers shift away from endpoint-only targeting and compromise management planes to control large asset populations.

Half of enterprises now run agentic AI in network operations

Cisco · Oct 7, 2026 · Research

Cisco and Omdia released research showing 75% of enterprises have deployed AI for network operations, with 51% running agentic AI in production today. 84% expect an AI-led operating model within 12 months. Key finding: 67% say generative AI increased network complexity; 95% say existing AIOps tools fall short significantly.

Why it matters Quantifies agentic AI adoption in NetOps: half of large enterprises already have agents acting in production; shows operators prioritize autonomy over advisory tools despite organizational barriers.

Cisco's September 2026 research conducted with Omdia surveyed 500+ employee organizations across North America, Western Europe, and Asia-Pacific on agentic AI adoption in network operations. Key findings: 75% have deployed some form of AI for network operations, and critically, 51% report running agentic AI systems that act in production today. 84% expect an AI-led operating model within 12 months. The research reveals a paradox: while 67% of respondents say the generative AI boom has significantly increased network complexity, and 70% report AI adoption has increased rather than decreased NetOps workloads, 95% say their existing non-agentic AIOps tools fall short in significant ways. Four in five organizations are comfortable granting agents high or fully autonomous roles. The research indicates the autonomy consensus has already shifted—the debate over whether AI should help run networks has effectively ended; the focus is now on governance, trust frameworks, and safe deployment.

An intelligent routing function matches workloads with suitable AI models to help enterprises optimize cost and performance across different models. The service integrates with Singtel's AI data centers, terrestrial and subsea connectivity, and Paragon orchestration platform, complementing existing GPU-as-a-Service offerings. China's three main telcos are ahead in token revenue streams, making Singtel an AI-native telco pioneer in Southeast Asia.

Mistral trains trillion-parameter model on European Blackwell cluster

TechCrunch · Oct 6, 2026 · Industry news

Mistral AI released Mistral Large 4, a 1-trillion-parameter natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 49 billion active parameters, trained from scratch on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's own European datacenters.

Why it matters The API is live at $1.36/$4.18 per million input/output tokens with weights shipping end of October—a new sovereign EU-based frontier model option competing directly with US and Chinese closed models at mid-tier pricing.

Mistral announced Large 4 (nicknamed Le Chonk) on October 6 as a public preview with API access live immediately and open weights promised by end of October. The model is a granular mixture-of-experts with hybrid instruct-and-reasoning capabilities and native image input, achieving only 4.7% weight activation per token despite the 1.05T parameter total—enabling competitive pricing for trillion-scale inference. CEO Arthur Mensch emphasized cybersecurity performance at a UAE conference, claiming the model exceeds Chinese open models on cyber tasks where closed models often refuse. Preliminary benchmarks show 62% on DeepSWE v1.1, 67% on FinWorkBench, 73% on DIOR-RSVG, though first independent evaluations suggest it is not the strongest general-purpose model. Training consumed approximately two months on Mistral's own European hardware, positioning it as a third way between closed US models and Chinese open-weights options.

GSA issues AI acquisition clause for federal contracts

FedScoop · Oct 6, 2026 · Industry news

The General Services Administration issued a regulation on AI acquisitions as a deviation on October 6, 2026, allowing immediate use for new contracts before it becomes mandatory on October 19. Federal agencies began purchasing ChatGPT through consumption-based token pricing under a OneGov agreement, replacing the previous $1 monthly model with 50% discount, creating access for approximately 23 million public servants.

Why it matters Establishes binding procurement standards for federal AI acquisitions and shifts US government from subscription to consumption-based AI purchasing—affecting budget forecasting and vendor relationships for agencies and contractors.

The GSA's October 6 release marks a critical milestone in federal AI governance. The regulation deviation enables immediate adoption for new contracts before formal rulemaking concludes on October 19, reducing friction for agencies implementing AI at scale. The OneGov agreement with OpenAI, effective this month, replaces flat-rate subscription pricing with a token-consumption model at 50% discount, fundamentally changing how agencies forecast and allocate AI spending. The move covers all 23 million public-sector employees across federal, state, and local government, creating standardized procurement terms that reduce negotiation overhead and establish baseline security, audit, and compliance requirements across government deployments. However, stakeholders flagged concerns about implementation clarity and long-term cost predictability under variable consumption models—particularly for agencies without mature AI governance infrastructure. The regulation applies immediately to all new solicitations and becomes mandatory on October 19, giving existing contracts a transition window.
