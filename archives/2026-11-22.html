Nornir 3.7 alpha adds async tasks and cancellable runs

OpsMill · Nov 16, 2026 · Primary source

Nornir 3.7.0a1 adds native asyncio runner, async task execution, run cancellation, progress tracking, and async inventory loading. Existing threaded code remains unaffected; ThreadedRunner is still default.

Why it matters Enables Nornir integration with async applications and modern transports without forcing migration; critical for teams building event-loop-based automation infrastructure.

Nornir 3.7.0a1 addresses a core limitation: threaded runners block event loops when embedded in async applications, and async connection libraries had nowhere to plug into execution. The release introduces three distinct features: (1) an opt-in asyncio runner via await nr.arun() for tasks running on the event loop; (2) execution control—nr.stream() to follow progress per host, nr.submit() for background runs with handle.cancel(), and timeout support; (3) async inventory loading via await AInitNornir() that doesn't block the loop. The release also enforces connection mode consistency: if a sync task tries to use an async connection (or vice versa), Nornir raises ConnectionModeMismatchError, preventing subtle bugs. Existing code using ThreadedRunner works unchanged. Two companion alpha releases enable testing: nornir-scrapli 1.6.0a1 for async device tasks and nornir-infrahub 1.3.0a2 for async inventory loading from Infrahub. The APIs remain subject to change before stable 3.7.0; this is explicitly an early release for community feedback.

Latent gateway defect and lost capacity caused Microsoft outage

Control Plane · Nov 19, 2026 · Analysis

Five widely reported outages hit major organizations between November 12 and November 16, 2026, with no organization or official reporting evidence of a cyberattack. Microsoft's preliminary report identified three factors: a latent defect in a July 2026 update to its networking gateway servers, extra load after Teams traffic shifted onto the gateway used by Exchange Online, and nearly 14% of gateway capacity already offline.

Why it matters The outage reveals how latent routing defects interact with traffic failovers and capacity constraints to trigger cascading failures across multiple services—a critical architectural lesson for operators managing complex multi-service, geographically distributed networks.

Microsoft's preliminary post-incident report, dated 15 November 2026, named three factors: a latent defect in a July 2026 fix to its networking gateway servers, extra load after a Teams failover, and nearly 14% of gateway capacity out of service. On 12 November at 01:07 UTC a routing issue on the gateway in the US East region caused Teams connection timeouts, after which Teams automatically failed over to the gateway that also serves Exchange Online. About 3.9 million US reports were attributed to Microsoft's disruption in the 60 hours following its start; roughly two-thirds of the weekly problem reports came from one initiating event. The incident exemplifies how modern cloud architectures depend on graceful failover of routing decisions, but when the fallback path is already capacity-constrained with existing defects, automatic failover can amplify rather than mitigate the fault. Microsoft's report identified a problem that prevented ordinary isolation between gateway partitions from containing the cascade, though no other reported November outage was linked to this vulnerability, and shows how a dormant defect can survive months of normal operation and turn a routine failover into a global cascade.

Chinese group exploited vCenter bypass for two months before patch

SecurityWeek · Nov 19, 2026 · Industry news

A China-linked espionage group exploited a critical VMware vCenter authentication bypass (CVE-2026-123890, CVSS 9.8) in the VMware Directory Service for more than two months before Broadcom released a patch on November 19. Attackers used custom tools Bonesaw and Sawsnake to steal encryption keys, forge SAML tokens, and bypass MFA.

Why it matters vCenter is a privileged control plane; patching removes the bypass but does not revoke forged SAML capability or added SSO admins—requiring immediate forensic triage, compromise hunting, and inventory of all vCenter instances for signs of lateral movement into ESXi infrastructure.

The suspected China-nexus cyber espionage group Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221) exploited CVE-2026-123890 in early September 2026. The flaw affects the VMware Directory Service in vCenter Server and allows an unauthenticated attacker with network access to bypass authentication and establish an administrator@vsphere.local session. Attackers deployed Bonesaw, a Golang-based exploitation tool, to target vCenter, then Sawsnake to enable Golden SAML attacks, forge authentication tokens, bypass multi-factor authentication, and impersonate any user. Broadcom patched the vulnerability on November 19 in VMSA-2026-0009, which also fixes four other vulnerabilities affecting VMware SDDC Manager and VMware Tools for Windows. Rapid7 opened a Metasploit pull request on November 20 with working exploit code expected by November 23. Infrastructure and virtualization teams must assume compromise on affected vCenter instances deployed before November 19 and conduct immediate forensics to detect SSO account creation, stolen encryption keys, master encryption key access, and lateral movement into ESXi hosts and Active Directory.

Critical OpenSSL overflow disputed and still unfixed

DeployBase · Nov 20, 2026 · Analysis

A heap buffer overflow in OpenSSL's Base64 encoding function (CVE-2026-121481, published November 18, CVSS 9.8) can corrupt application memory when processing very large inputs. CISA scored it 9.8 on November 19, but OpenSSL's own assessment is Low, and no fixed release exists—only an unmerged pull request.

Why it matters For large untrusted input, EVP_EncodeBlock() integer overflow causes writes beyond buffer bounds that crash applications or corrupt memory. No patch is available yet, forcing teams to identify which applications invoke Base64 encoding with external data and plan for rapid deployment once a release appears.

The flaw affects EVP_EncodeBlock(), which converts binary data into printable Base64 text; it produces four output bytes per three input bytes with padding. The application supplies the buffer that holds the result. For very large inputs, OpenSSL's calculation of the encoded length exceeds the integer range (CWE-190, integer overflow), causing the encoder to write beyond the supplied buffer boundary, corrupting nearby memory or crashing the application. OpenSSL is the CVE numbering authority for its own CVEs and rated this Low based on its security policy, but NVD/CISA enrichment assigned 9.8 on November 19 based on remote network exploitability with no privileges required. The fix is pull request 33652, opened November 10 and approved by November 20, but no release date has been announced. This creates a dangerous window: teams see 9.8 headlines and cannot patch because no release exists. Network appliances and servers using OpenSSL for large Base64-encoded payloads (file uploads, API responses, encoding large binary data) are at risk; teams should monitor OpenSSL releases closely and prepare to apply immediately.

CISA flags exploited VMware, Cisco and Apache flaws

The Hacker News · Nov 21, 2026 · Industry news

CISA added four actively exploited flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 21: CVE-2026-123890 (VMware vCenter authentication bypass, CVSS 9.8), CVE-2026-129448 (Apache Roller XML-RPC deserialization RCE, CVSS 9.8), CVE-2026-76466 (Cisco Expressway hardcoded credentials and root command execution, CVSS 9.8), and Cisco Expressway hardcoded credentials flaw exploited against Russian organizations.

Why it matters All three products are infrastructure-critical and mass-exploitable; CVE-2026-76466 was weaponized to breach a Russian organization after PoC release. Federal agencies must patch by November 23. Network teams running externally accessible VMware vCenter, Cisco Expressway, or Apache Roller instances face immediate active threat and must patch and audit logs for compromise indicators.

CVE-2026-123890 is the VMware vCenter authentication bypass exploited by Armory Castle espionage group as a zero-day since September 2026 (see separate article). CVE-2026-129448 affects Apache Roller and allows an unauthenticated remote attacker to execute arbitrary code via the XML-RPC endpoint through deserialization of untrusted data before authentication. CVE-2026-76466 affects Cisco Expressway Series and allows an unauthenticated remote attacker with persistent cookies and network access to execute commands as root on a clustered device via the database service using hardcoded credentials. The addition to KEV indicates all three are actively exploited in the wild with public working code or proofs-of-concept. Infrastructure and security teams running these products should activate incident response capabilities to scan for compromise before patching, especially in externally accessible systems. Organizations must assume reconnaissance and exploitation attempts across these surfaces.

Postgres read-replica failure took down ChatGPT for hours

DEV Community / OpenAI Status · Nov 22, 2026 · Primary source

On November 21, ChatGPT experienced a 3.5-hour outage with failure rates peaking at 75%, triggered by scheduled maintenance on a PostgreSQL service that caused both read replicas to fail simultaneously. The Primary API service handles user and service-to-service authentication across OpenAI's entire stack, so read replica failure cascaded across all dependent request paths.

Why it matters The postmortem's remediation list clarifies failure modes in distributed auth layers; the outage timing ensures operational lessons reach infrastructure planners beyond ops teams.

Between 1:48 and 5:21 PT on November 21, OpenAI suffered a major outage affecting 75% of ChatGPT requests on both free and paid tiers. The root cause was routine: scheduled maintenance on a PostgreSQL database service used by OpenAI's Primary API service caused both read replicas to fail at the same time. The Primary API service provides user authentication and service-to-service authentication across the entire OpenAI stack, so when both read replicas went offline and their lag grew without bound, every request path depending on them failed. The postmortem's remediation list is substantive enough to earn attention beyond the operations community; the outage coincided with a Nasdaq 100 decline of 5.61% and the index's worst week in six years, guaranteeing the postmortem gets read by infrastructure and reliability teams planning for similar cascading failure modes in distributed authentication systems. The incident illustrates a critical failure pattern: in systems where database read replicas handle authentication, maintenance windows without proper failover can disable the entire service layer.

Regulators across five jurisdictions issue AI agent guidance

Beyond Horizons · Nov 20, 2026 · Analysis

Between 14 October and 18 November 2026, official bodies in Singapore, Hong Kong, the United Kingdom, the European Union and Australia published guidance on AI agents. The EU AI Act now includes templates for fundamental rights impact assessments, Australia announced mandatory safety protections with a new regulator, and Hong Kong's central bank seeks views on registering material AI agents in banking.

Why it matters Standards bodies identified a control problem: AI agents connected to business systems hold delegated authority over credentials and configurations, yet most deployments cannot attribute agent actions or reliably halt them.

Regulatory bodies across five major jurisdictions released AI agent governance frameworks within a 5-week window, signaling coordinated urgency around autonomous systems deployed to financial and public infrastructure. The EU's impact-assessment template operationalizes its AI Act; Australia's announcement establishes a dedicated regulator for frontier-AI safety; Hong Kong's HKMA discussion paper follows its October 'ART' framework (Accountability, Resilience, Trust) and seeks a registry of material AI agents in banking. Singapore's joint advisory from CSA, GovTech, and IMDA covers safe deployment of personal AI agents. The core governance challenge: agents hold delegated authority over business credentials and configurations, yet current deployments cannot reliably attribute agent actions or halt them when control fails. This gap between capability and observability shapes compliance burden for enterprises deploying agentic systems to production environments touching critical transactions or regulated workflows.
